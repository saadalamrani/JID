# JID CMO Strategy Report 2026

**Role:** Chief Marketing Officer
**Decision date:** 2026-08-25
**Evidence convention:** FACT = directly supported; RESEARCH FINDING = published analysis; MARKET SIGNAL = directional observation; HYPOTHESIS = requires validation; OPINION = role judgment; RECOMMENDATION = proposed action.

## 1. Executive Summary

**RECOMMENDATION:** Position JID as Saudi Arabia's trusted career-evidence and opportunity operating system—not as another job board and not as a LinkedIn clone. Win a narrow wedge by making career evidence reusable, opportunity discovery explainable, and employer/university outcomes measurable. Marketing must earn trust through proof, Arabic-first usefulness, and partner-led distribution.

## 2. CMO Mandate

Define the category, audience promises, acquisition loops, lifecycle communication, launch gates, and evidence standards. Growth claims remain subordinate to user rights, source traceability, and actual product readiness.

## 3. What I Learned

**FACT:** The source corpus shows broad ambition but recurring confusion between actor, customer, beneficiary, feature, and market. **RESEARCH FINDING:** Saudi policy sources emphasize skills, participation, data protection, and accountable digital services. **OPINION:** JID's best marketing asset is not reach; it is a credible evidence contract.

## 4. Saudi Current State

**FACT:** Jadarat is a national employment platform; HRDF and Human Capability Development Program initiatives already occupy important employment and capability terrain. JID must complement them. **HYPOTHESIS:** Saudi individuals will value one Arabic-first record that turns education, projects, work samples, and verified experiences into reusable applications.

## 5. Global Benchmark

LinkedIn owns professional identity/network distribution; Handshake owns university-employer network effects in major markets; ATS vendors own hiring workflow; assessment vendors own point tools. **RECOMMENDATION:** Do not attack all four categories. Market the connective tissue: evidence that travels across transitions.

## 6. Jobs to Be Done

- Individual: "Help me present truthful evidence, find relevant opportunities, and know what to do next."
- Business: "Help me find and evaluate candidates consistently without creating more admin."
- University: "Help me support transitions and report outcomes with transparent coverage."

## 7. Pain Points

Fragmented profiles, repetitive applications, opaque rankings, low-confidence screening, unverifiable claims, weak Arabic journeys, and outcome dashboards whose denominators or sources are unclear.

## 8. Unmet Needs

An evidence-bearing Career Record; explainable opportunity discovery; private action tracking; structured, auditable hiring; and institution-level outcomes whose methodology is visible.

## 9. Market Structure

JID sits between public infrastructure, commercial talent platforms, institutions, employers, and individuals. It should be a complement where sovereign or institutional platforms are authoritative, a workflow product where coordination is missing, and a differentiated evidence layer where portability is weak.

## 10. Competitors and Substitutes

Direct substitutes include job boards, LinkedIn, ATS products, university career portals, spreadsheets, WhatsApp, email, and manual career-service workflows. **OPINION:** The most dangerous competitor is the low-cost workaround, not a named platform.

## 11. Opportunities

Own "career evidence" as a category; create a high-trust Arabic-first brand; use employers and universities as credibility channels; build transition-specific campaigns; publish methodology notes as marketing assets.

## 12. Risks

Brand overpromise, invented outcome claims, implying government endorsement, marketing an unvalidated match score, spam acquisition, paid visibility that corrupts relevance, and social features that reward public performance over progress.

## 13. What JID Should Do

Lead with the Career Record, Lammah-powered opportunity intelligence, Abhathli guidance, Radar tracking, and structured employer workflows. Publish the provenance and limitation of every headline metric.

## 14. What JID Should Not Do

Do not promise jobs, publish fabricated adoption numbers, imply official status, sell candidate attention, launch mass auto-apply, or use "AI" as the primary value proposition. Do not buy growth before retention and evidence quality are demonstrated.

## 15. Product and Service Implications

Every acquisition page should map to a completed user job, not a feature list. Product surfaces need shareable evidence artifacts, finite guidance, transparent recommendations, and clear next actions.

## 16. Data Implications

Define acquisition, activation, evidence completion, opportunity action, interview progression, and outcome events. Separate observed events from self-report and partner-supplied data. Never turn missing data into a positive outcome.

## 17. Technology Implications

Support campaign attribution without cross-context behavioral exploitation; consented messaging preferences; bilingual structured content; server-side entitlements; and analytics events with documented meaning.

## 18. Privacy and Legal Implications

Consent must be purpose-specific; withdrawal usable; employer access visible; partner data-sharing disclosed. Claims involving government, outcomes, or employment probability require legal and evidence review.

## 19. Business Model

Keep the Individual core record free. Charge for recurring advanced guidance only after free value is proven. Employer and University subscriptions fund workflow and measurement; implementation is priced separately. No data sale and no pay-to-win organic ranking.

## 20. Partnerships

Recruit 3–5 employer design partners and one university design partner with explicit learning contracts. Build referral partnerships only when they add verified inventory, evidence, or outcomes—not logo decoration.

## 21. Validation Plan

Test three positioning messages with real task completion, not clicks alone: Career Record, opportunity guidance, and structured hiring. Require qualitative interviews, activation cohorts, repeat-use data, and willingness-to-pay evidence before scale.

## 22. First 90 Days

Weeks 1–2: message and claim audit. Weeks 3–6: design-partner recruitment and bilingual landing tests. Weeks 7–10: cohort onboarding and lifecycle experiments. Weeks 11–13: retention, trust, and willingness-to-pay review; publish only substantiated results.

## 23. Twelve-Month Direction

Build a partner-led Saudi category narrative, repeatable university/employer launch playbooks, transition campaigns, and a public methodology center. Expand channels only after the wedge retains.

## 24. Three-Year Direction

JID becomes the recognized evidence and transition layer connecting individuals, employers, and universities in Saudi Arabia, with selective regional expansion after Arabic-first trust and economics are proven.

## 25. Saudi Leadership Path

Lead through Arabic product depth, PDPL-aware trust, transparent outcomes, locally relevant evidence models, and complementarity with public institutions. Saudi leadership is demonstrated by usefulness and accountability, not nationalism in copy.

## 26. Global Leadership Path

Export a portable, consented Career Record and explainable transition workflow to markets where professional evidence is fragmented. Global credibility requires interoperability and published methodology.

## 27. Disagreements and Dissent

I dissent from broad social launch in the first year; it would dilute the evidence category and raise moderation cost. I also dissent from a premium Individual paywall around the canonical record. I support bounded professional contribution only after the transition wedge works.

## 28. Founder Decisions Required

Approve category statement; approve no job-guarantee/no official-endorsement claims; choose the first design-partner segment; approve free-core boundary; decide whether social is deferred or limited to finite, purpose-bound formats.

## 29. Research Appendix

Open questions: which transition segment has strongest urgency; whether Career Record or Abhathli leads acquisition; employer willingness to pay for structured evidence; university budget owner; Arabic terminology comprehension; acceptable notification frequency.
