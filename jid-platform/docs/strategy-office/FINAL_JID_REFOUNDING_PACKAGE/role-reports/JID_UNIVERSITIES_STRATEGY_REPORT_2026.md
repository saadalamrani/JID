# JID Universities Strategy Report 2026

**Role:** Universities Director
**Study date:** 2026-08-25
**Status:** Independent role recommendation; not founder-adopted; no implementation authority
**Evidence labels:** `FACT`, `RESEARCH FINDING`, `MARKET SIGNAL`, `HYPOTHESIS`, `OPINION`, `RECOMMENDATION`

## 1. Executive Summary

**[RECOMMENDATION]** JID should not sell universities “another employability dashboard.” It should offer a managed **Graduate Transition Evidence & Intervention Service** that helps an institution define cohorts, obtain lawful and honest coverage, coordinate career-service actions, collect employer feedback, and produce decision-ready reports whose denominators and limitations are visible.

**[FACT]** The Saudi university outcomes category is not empty. The National Labor Observatory (NLO) publishes graduate-employment analyses by university, including employment, wages, waiting time to first job, on-the-job training, and job-seeker indicators. ETEC’s 2026 higher-education instruments recognize employment, job-major alignment, salary, employer assessment, and graduate readiness. [S01–S04] JID therefore must complement official outcome infrastructure, not claim to create it.

The initial paid wedge should be a 12–16 week service-supported pilot for one institution and a bounded set of programs. The pilot should produce: a cohort/data-readiness audit; consent/legal-basis and access map; graduate evidence-completion workflow; career intervention queue; employer-feedback workflow; and one coverage-honest decision report. **[HYPOTHESIS]** Universities will pay when JID reduces reporting labor and enables visible action before and after graduation—not merely because it can draw charts.

No institutional outcome percentage should appear until the eligible population, numerator, denominator, observation window, missingness, exclusions, and source are defined. Missing is not zero; platform participation is not a representative cohort; student service usage is not employment impact; and JID pilot data are not national statistics.

## 2. Mandate

The Universities Director represents the needs of university leadership, career centers, institutional research and quality teams, program leaders, faculty, IT/data governance, employer-relations teams, students, and alumni without collapsing them into one “university user.” The role must determine:

- which institutional job is urgent and paid;
- which official outcomes and quality frameworks JID must align with;
- what data the institution lawfully controls or can request;
- where JID adds workflow and intervention value rather than duplicating NLO/ETEC;
- who buys, approves, operates, and is accountable;
- what minimum evidence makes a pilot and later benchmark defensible.

The mandate does not authorize JID to expose private graduate records, infer institutional performance from JID users, or imply accreditation, ranking, or government authority.

## 3. What I Learned

**[FACT]** NLO already provides an official graduate-outcomes layer. Its published university report covers 55 public and private institutions for a stated cohort and observation window. [S01]

**[FACT]** ETEC’s Saudi Global Ranking Guide weights labor-market alignment dimensions, while its employer survey instrument asks about readiness, qualification/job fit, knowledge application, and job-major alignment. These are recognized constructs, not proof that any one university has a problem or that JID can calculate the result. [S02–S03]

**[FACT]** An earlier University Affairs Council/MOE direction called for universities to measure and publish employment by specialty, field alignment, job type, and salary. Its current 2026 implementation status requires confirmation. [S04]

**[RESEARCH FINDING]** GASTAT’s Q1 2025 survey shows unemployed Saudis use multiple search channels, including direct employer contact, Jadarat, and professional/social networks. University career services should therefore help graduates coordinate evidence and action across channels rather than lock them to a campus-only board. [S05]

**[MARKET SIGNAL]** The internal JID package treats universities as a commercial wedge, but it supplies no current buyer interviews, procurement map, implementation budget, paid pilot, or representative university data. Commercial confidence is therefore provisional.

## 4. Saudi Current State

Saudi universities operate inside a developed human-capability, employment, quality, and data-governance ecosystem:

- **[FACT]** Vision 2030/HCDP connects education, future labor-market readiness, graduate employment, career pathways, and employer participation. The cited HCDP delivery plan covers 2021–2025 and must not be presented as an unchanged 2026 work plan. [S06–S07]
- **[FACT]** NLO maintains national labor-force and graduate-employment analysis. [S01, S08]
- **[FACT]** ETEC defines higher-education ranking and employer-assessment constructs relevant to labor-market alignment. [S02–S03]
- **[FACT]** MHRSD describes a Saudi Skills Taxonomy connecting occupations, sectors, skills, and education. [S09]
- **[FACT]** HRDF operates Jadarat and Tamheer, creating official opportunity and transition pathways that a university service should source accurately. [S10–S12]
- **[FACT]** PDPL rights and controller-accountability duties constrain collection, disclosure, retention, and reuse of student and graduate data. [S13–S15]

**[OPINION]** The strategic space is the gap between national outcomes reporting and daily institutional action: who needs help, what evidence is incomplete, which employer relationship should be activated, which program sees repeated feedback, and whether an intervention was actually completed.

## 5. Global Benchmark

**[RESEARCH FINDING]** HESA’s Graduate Outcomes model demonstrates that graduate outcomes require a defined survey population, collection timing, methodology, and quality treatment; presentation without those foundations can mislead. [S16]

**[RESEARCH FINDING]** NACE’s First-Destination Standards demonstrate the importance of a defined graduating class, career-outcome categories, knowledge rate, and reporting window. [S17]

**[MARKET SIGNAL]** Handshake demonstrates that an institution-linked early-career network can combine student identity, employers, opportunities, and career services. Its US market position and data access are not transferable evidence that the same commercial model works in Saudi Arabia. [S18]

The lesson is not to copy a US campus network. The lesson is to combine methodological discipline with Saudi-native institutional workflows, Arabic/English evidence, official-source alignment, and explicit data rights.

## 6. User/Customer Jobs-to-be-Done

- **University president/vice president:** know whether graduate-transition strategy is working and where executive action is required.
- **Quality/institutional research:** produce defensible, reproducible indicators with disclosed coverage and definitions.
- **Career center:** identify who needs intervention, coordinate services, engage employers, and close follow-up loops.
- **Program leader/dean:** see repeated capability and employer-feedback themes relevant to curriculum and co-curricular action.
- **Employer-relations team:** manage target employers, opportunities, events, internships, feedback, and conversion.
- **IT/data governance/privacy:** understand data flow, authority, role, retention, security, export, and deletion obligations.
- **Procurement/finance/legal:** assess scope, value, liability, vendor risk, and contract terms.
- **Student/graduate:** receive useful service without losing control of private career information or becoming a data point in an opaque score.

## 7. Pain Points

1. Outcome records are distributed across official sources, surveys, career-center tools, spreadsheets, faculty contacts, employer communications, and alumni channels.
2. Cohort definitions and observation windows can differ, making superficially similar metrics incomparable.
3. Self-selected platform users create coverage bias.
4. Career-service events are easy to count but hard to connect responsibly to transition outcomes.
5. Employer feedback is often unstructured, low-volume, and vulnerable to anecdotal overreach.
6. Students repeat identity, CV, and application work across university and external systems.
7. Institutional teams can spend time compiling reports after the fact instead of acting early.
8. Data-sharing expectations among university, JID, graduate, employer, and public institution may be unclear.

## 8. Unmet Needs

- A cohort and metric-definition registry with immutable versions.
- A data-readiness scorecard that shows gaps without inventing performance.
- Consent/legal-basis and disclosure workflows at the point of use.
- A graduate evidence-completion and transition-action workflow.
- Employer feedback linked to a defined role/program/context rather than generalized reputation.
- Intervention queues with ownership, due dates, outcome, and audit trail.
- Program-level reporting with coverage and small-cell protections.
- Source-aligned opportunity and official program discovery.
- A clear boundary between official national statistics and JID operational evidence.

## 9. Market Structure

University employability is a buying committee and service market, not a single-user SaaS sale. A likely economic buyer may sit in leadership, quality, career services, transformation, or institutional research; the daily operator may be elsewhere; IT, procurement, privacy, legal, and finance can each block adoption.

**[HYPOTHESIS]** A founder-led private university or an institution with a named transition/reporting pain may move faster than a large public procurement. That is a segmentation hypothesis, not a rule.

The viable entry is likely services plus bounded software. Pure consulting does not compound; pure software arrives before definitions, permissions, and workflow ownership are ready.

## 10. Competitors/Substitutes

- NLO official graduate-employment and labor-market reports. [S01, S08]
- ETEC quality/ranking instruments and institutional reporting processes. [S02–S03]
- University SIS, data warehouse, CRM, LMS, survey tools, BI tools, and career-service platforms.
- Jadarat, employer career sites, LinkedIn, and other opportunity channels.
- Consulting firms conducting graduate surveys, accreditation preparation, or strategy work.
- Spreadsheets, email, WhatsApp, alumni offices, faculty contacts, and manual employer surveys.

**[RECOMMENDATION]** JID should integrate or reference authoritative sources and replace fragile coordination work—not displace systems of record without a proven need.

## 11. Opportunities

1. **Data Readiness Diagnostic:** cohort inventory, definitions, source map, permissions, coverage, gaps, and risk.
2. **Transition Operations:** evidence-completion campaigns, service routing, application/action tracking, and follow-up.
3. **Employer Alignment Loop:** structured program/role-specific feedback, opportunities, events, and conversion evidence.
4. **Coverage-Honest Reporting:** reproducible institutional reports with traceable claims.
5. **Program Intelligence:** recurring evidence themes and intervention outcomes, with minimum sample/coverage gates.
6. **Alumni Re-engagement:** purposeful career support and outcomes refresh, not a vanity community feed.
7. **Institutional Career Record:** a student-controlled record that can persist beyond graduation without becoming university property.

## 12. Risks

- Decorative dashboards without sufficient data.
- Misrepresenting JID users as a full cohort.
- Publishing small-cell or identifiable data.
- Treating missing responses as unemployment or zero.
- Causal claims about career services from observational participation.
- Duplicate or inconsistent official metrics.
- University access continuing after its lawful purpose ends.
- Employer feedback becoming discriminatory, defamatory, or statistically meaningless.
- Long procurement and implementation cycles consuming runway.
- Custom work fragmenting the platform.
- Ranking institutions or programs before methodology is defensible.
- Students feeling surveilled or coerced into disclosure.

## 13. What JID Should Do

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Sell a bounded outcome: “Within one pilot cohort, JID will establish a lawful data and metric baseline, operate selected career interventions, and deliver a reproducible report showing coverage, actions, and unresolved gaps.”

Start with a program family or graduating cohort small enough for operational learning but large enough to test coverage. Pair the university with two or three employers relevant to that cohort. Preserve individual control over private evidence. Use NLO/ETEC/MHRSD definitions or explicit crosswalks where they fit; never rename a JID metric to sound official.

## 14. What JID Should Not Do

- Lead with a generic dashboard demo.
- Promise accreditation, ranking improvement, employment uplift, or representative benchmarking.
- Duplicate NLO and call it innovation.
- expose named students or graduates to broad faculty/leadership access.
- use platform participation as the denominator for “graduate employment rate” when the eligible cohort is larger.
- create a universal readiness/employability score.
- infer field alignment, salary, or employment without a declared source and method.
- make students pay for access to core institutional career services.
- require a clean-slate replacement of SIS, CRM, LMS, BI, or official systems.

## 15. Product/Service Implications

The university offering should contain six bounded capabilities:

1. **Institution Workspace:** roles, programs, cohorts, data agreements, metric versions, and audit.
2. **Data Readiness:** source inventory, cohort reconciliation, missingness, coverage, and issue queue.
3. **Transition Campaigns:** consent/notice, evidence completion, appointments, events, employer actions, and follow-up.
4. **Employer Exchange:** verified employer contacts, structured feedback instruments, opportunities, internships, and closure.
5. **Outcomes Report Builder:** approved measures, denominators, coverage, suppression, methodology notes, and export.
6. **Action Board:** owner, intervention, due date, completion, evidence, and learning review.

The product should show a coverage warning before any metric and make “not enough evidence” a first-class result.

## 16. Data Implications

University data should be separated into:

- institutional reference data: programs, departments, cohorts, academic terms;
- individual career data controlled according to defined roles and purposes;
- service events: appointment, workshop, evidence completion, referral, application support;
- opportunity/employer events: invitation, application, interview, offer, placement, closure;
- outcome assertions: employment, further study, entrepreneurship, seeking work, other—each with source and time;
- aggregate projections with coverage and suppression rules.

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Every measure must store: definition version, eligible population, numerator, denominator, source, owner, collection method, legal basis, observation window, update date, missingness, exclusions, confidence/limitation, identifiability, and correction owner.

No institution should receive a broad mirror of the Individual’s Career Record. It receives purpose-specific fields and aggregates under defined permissions.

## 17. Technology Implications Where Relevant

- Preserve institution/organization identity separation and tenant isolation.
- Use role- and purpose-based access, not only coarse organization membership.
- Maintain metric definitions as versioned configuration, not hard-coded labels.
- Create import validation, reconciliation, quarantine, and rollback for institutional files.
- Record provenance at field/event level.
- Support Arabic-first and English parity, including program and occupation crosswalks.
- Implement small-cell suppression and export policy before analytics release.
- Keep official-source connectors read-only until licensing, API authority, and data-use terms are documented.
- Do not use an LLM to “clean” outcome data without retaining source, transformation, and human approval.

## 18. Privacy/Legal Implications Where Relevant

**[FACT]** PDPL establishes data-subject rights and controller accountability, purpose limitation, minimization, accuracy, and retention duties. [S13–S15]

Before a pilot, university and JID must document:

- controller/processor or controller-to-controller roles by processing purpose;
- legal basis for each collection, disclosure, matching, survey, and report;
- whether participation is voluntary and how refusal affects service;
- notice, access, correction, deletion/destruction, export, objection, and complaint workflows as applicable;
- retention and post-graduation transitions;
- processor/subprocessor, hosting, transfer, breach, and audit terms;
- sensitive data and high-risk profiling exclusions;
- small-cell, re-identification, and publication controls.

Consent must be specific where used; it is not the only possible basis and cannot cure an unnecessary purpose. Legal counsel must scope the actual institution and data flow.

## 19. Business Model

### Recommended entry offer

- Fixed-fee Data Readiness Diagnostic.
- Fixed-scope 12–16 week Transition Evidence Pilot.
- Optional managed survey/employer-feedback service.
- Conversion to annual platform-and-service agreement only after operational value is shown.

### Pricing logic

Price by cohort/program scope, workflow volume, implementation effort, reporting complexity, integrations, and service level—not by how many colorful KPIs appear. Do not charge based on individual outcome or sell graduate data.

**[HYPOTHESIS]** A paid diagnostic can qualify seriousness and fund discovery better than a free dashboard pilot. This requires buyer testing.

## 20. Partnerships

- One pilot university with leadership, career center, quality/research, IT/privacy, procurement, and program sponsorship.
- Two or three verified employers aligned to the pilot cohort.
- NLO/ETEC/MOE dialogue for terminology and non-duplication; no data access presumed.
- MHRSD/HRDF links for official skills and programs where authorized.
- Assessment providers only when instruments are validated for the use, language, and population.
- Independent methodology/privacy reviewers for the first institutional report.

## 21. Validation Needed

1. Which role owns the problem and budget?
2. What report or intervention currently consumes the most labor?
3. What is the eligible cohort and which systems identify it?
4. What proportion of outcomes can be known lawfully and reproducibly?
5. Which decisions change when a metric changes?
6. Can the university contact graduates and share selected data with JID?
7. What current vendor/consultant/process is the substitute?
8. Is a fixed-fee diagnostic purchasable within 90 days?
9. Will employers provide structured, contextual feedback?
10. Do students perceive the workflow as useful and controllable?

Pilot success should measure readiness issues resolved, coverage improvement, reporting labor saved, intervention completion, employer response, user trust, and decision usefulness—not invented employment uplift.

## 22. 90-Day Actions

### Days 1–15

- Create the institutional discovery guide and buyer map.
- Define the metric/data/coverage contract.
- Prepare a no-data product walkthrough and pilot scope.
- Obtain legal/privacy review of the proposed data flow.

### Days 16–45

- Conduct 5–8 institutional buying-unit studies.
- Audit one candidate cohort workflow using synthetic or de-identified examples until agreement.
- Interview 2–3 relevant employers and 8–12 students/graduates.
- Select one paid diagnostic candidate; do not promise integration.

### Days 46–75

- Complete a documented data-readiness diagnostic.
- Agree metric definitions, roles, retention, access, and pilot success measures.
- Test intervention and report prototypes with actual operators.

### Days 76–90

- Deliver a founder go/no-go memo: buyer, budget path, data feasibility, legal risks, service cost, product reuse, and paid-pilot terms.
- If gates pass, contract the bounded pilot. If not, preserve research and stop institutional build.

## 23. 12-Month Roadmap

- **Q1:** discovery, diagnostic method, first paid diagnostic.
- **Q2:** one bounded pilot, manual-service instrumentation, coverage-honest report.
- **Q3:** repeat with a second program/institution only if definitions and workflows generalize; add minimal imports and action board.
- **Q4:** annual offer, role templates, reproducible report package, employer loop, and one approved integration if contractually justified.

No cross-university benchmark should launch in year one unless cohort definitions, coverage thresholds, comparability, and publication rights pass independent review.

## 24. 3-Year Direction

**[HYPOTHESIS]** JID can become a trusted university-to-work transition operations layer across Saudi institutions, connecting student-controlled career evidence, verified employers, services, and transparent outcome methodology.

Year 2 may add program-level employer learning, alumni refresh, and sector transition cohorts. Year 3 may add statistically responsible peer learning and international expansion of the methodology. Expansion must preserve the distinction between official national statistics and JID operational evidence.

## 25. Contribution to Saudi Leadership

JID could contribute by:

- operationalizing education-to-work support around real evidence;
- aligning institution action with Saudi skills and outcome language;
- helping career centers close follow-up loops;
- increasing transparency about coverage and missingness;
- enabling responsible employer feedback and internship/graduate pathways;
- supporting Arabic-first career evidence and regional/sector context.

**[FACT]** These are possible contributions, not government-recognized outcomes or authority.

## 26. Contribution to Global Potential

Global differentiation would come from a privacy-respecting transition model in which the individual keeps a persistent evidence record while institutions receive bounded service and outcome projections. The exportable asset is the methodology and workflow: cohort truth, evidence provenance, intervention operations, and coverage-honest reporting. It is not a Saudi-themed dashboard or an unverified ranking product.

## 27. Disagreements with Other Strategy Office Roles

1. **With CEO/CFO sequencing:** I disagree that university work should wait until after a fully proven employer product. A bounded paid diagnostic should start in the first 90 days because it tests a distinct buyer and distribution path. I agree that full dashboard build must wait.
2. **With CPO:** I reject making “university dashboard” the product noun. It encourages visual output before workflow and data readiness.
3. **With CDO:** I oppose centralizing all graduate data into a JID intelligence lake before purpose, role, cohort, and retention are contractually defined.
4. **With CMO:** I oppose claims that JID “solves graduate employability” or advances accreditation. The first credible promise is better evidence and action operations.
5. **With Government Relations:** I do not want the first pilot delayed until an official partnership exists. We can align terminology and seek dialogue while running a lawful private pilot.
6. **With COO:** I oppose unlimited customization for a prestigious institution. The pilot must test a reusable method, not become bespoke outsourcing.

## 28. Decisions Required from Founder

1. Approve or reject **Graduate Transition Evidence & Intervention Service** as the university proposition.
2. Authorize a paid diagnostic in the first 90 days, before a full platform build.
3. Decide whether one university pilot may run in parallel with the individual/employer proof loop.
4. Approve the rule that no outcome percentage appears without cohort, denominator, coverage, window, source, and limitation.
5. Approve “complement NLO/ETEC/MOE” as the institutional stance.
6. Approve a prohibition on selling individual graduate data or institutional paid ranking.
7. Set the maximum bespoke service capacity and acceptable pilot subsidy.
8. Authorize institutional outreach; this is not approval to sign data-sharing terms without legal review.

### 29.2 Learning rules

- A metric without a decision owner is removed.
- A low-coverage metric remains a gap, not a result.
- A survey construct is not a verified fact about a graduate.
- An employer comment is not program truth until contextualized and repeated.
- A service participant/nonparticipant comparison is not causal impact without an appropriate design.
- A benchmark is prohibited until comparability is demonstrated.
