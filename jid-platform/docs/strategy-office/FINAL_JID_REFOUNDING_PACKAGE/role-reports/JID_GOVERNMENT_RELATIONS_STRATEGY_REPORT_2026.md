# JID Government Relations Strategy Report 2026

**Role:** Government Relations Director
**Study date:** 2026-08-25
**Status:** Independent role recommendation; not founder-adopted; no authority to represent government relationships
**Evidence labels:** `FACT`, `RESEARCH FINDING`, `MARKET SIGNAL`, `HYPOTHESIS`, `OPINION`, `RECOMMENDATION`

## 1. Executive Summary

**[RECOMMENDATION]** JID’s government posture should be **complementary, specific, and earned**. It should not describe itself as an alternative to Jadarat, a national labor observatory, a government platform, or a Vision 2030 initiative. It should present a bounded private-sector proposition: better individual-controlled career evidence, verified opportunity/workflow closure, employer decision quality, and university intervention operations—using official definitions and sources where authorized.

**[FACT]** Saudi Arabia already has defined institutions for labor policy, public employment infrastructure, human-capability strategy, official statistics, labor-market observation, education quality, data governance, digital government, and cybersecurity. [S01–S14] A government strategy that treats those functions as a commercial gap would be inaccurate and politically weak.

The first 90 days should build an institutional map, claims protocol, public-sector data/partnership gate, and a small number of listening briefings. The objective is not a memorandum of understanding for publicity. It is to learn where JID can provide lawful incremental value without creating dependency on uncommitted data access.

**[HYPOTHESIS]** If JID proves a measurable private pilot—such as improved vacancy closure, graduate-intervention completion, or evidence quality—an institution may later contract, partner, refer, or allow governed data exchange. Government revenue and official integration should not be in the base-case financial plan until there is a named owner, procurement path, authority, budget, and written data/contract scope.

## 2. Mandate

The Government Relations Director must:

- map institutional mandates and avoid overlap claims;
- translate JID’s value into public-interest outcomes without overstating impact;
- determine which conversations require founder authorization;
- identify public-sector buyers, policy owners, platform operators, regulators, data authorities, and standards bodies separately;
- distinguish a listening meeting, endorsement, referral, pilot, procurement, data-sharing agreement, and formal partnership;
- protect JID from reputational, legal, procurement, and dependency risk.

This role may recommend outreach. It may not imply a government relationship, display official marks, announce alignment as endorsement, promise integration, or commit data/technology without founder, legal, security, and delivery approval.

## 3. What I Learned

**[FACT]** Jadarat is the unified national employment platform for public- and private-sector opportunities and is intended to standardize and verify jobseeker information. [S01]

**[FACT]** HRDF’s 2024 report records national-scale Jadarat activity and an advanced matching engine active since April 2024. [S02] JID cannot credibly pitch “the first Saudi job-matching platform.”

**[FACT]** NLO already publishes national labor-force and graduate-employment analysis; GASTAT publishes official labor statistics using survey and administrative methods. [S03–S06] JID pilot telemetry cannot be called national labor-market intelligence.

**[FACT]** MHRSD is building a Saudi Skills Taxonomy and sectoral standards, while the 2026 Nitaqat phase carries a stated three-year localization-opportunity target. [S07–S09]

**[FACT]** SDAIA, DGA, and NCA establish distinct data-governance, digital-government, and cybersecurity frameworks. [S10–S14] Following a public control document does not create certification or official acceptance.

**[MARKET SIGNAL]** The founder wants Saudi leadership and institutional relevance. That ambition is strategically compatible with official priorities, but relevance must be proven through outcomes and conduct—not slogans.

## 4. Saudi Current State

### Institutional landscape

- **Vision 2030/HCDP:** strategic human-capability and education-to-employment context. [S15–S16]
- **MHRSD:** labor policy, localization, skills/occupations, and regulatory context. [S07–S09]
- **HRDF/Jadarat:** employment and training programs and national employment-platform operations. [S01–S02, S17–S18]
- **GASTAT:** official statistics and statistical methodology. [S05–S06]
- **NLO:** labor-market analysis, graduate outcomes, and foresight. [S03–S04]
- **MOE/ETEC:** education governance, quality, ranking, and employer/graduate assessment context. [S19–S21]
- **SDAIA:** personal-data governance and PDPL framework. [S10–S12]
- **DGA:** government digital-policy and enterprise-architecture expectations. [S13]
- **NCA:** national cybersecurity controls and sector/scope requirements. [S14]

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Maintain a living mandate map. A single organization can be policy owner, operator, buyer, regulator, data holder, standards setter, or partner in different contexts; those roles must not be conflated.

## 5. Global Benchmark

**[RESEARCH FINDING]** OECD digital-government guidance emphasizes coherent, user-driven, data-enabled public services and governance rather than isolated technology procurement. [S22]

**[RESEARCH FINDING]** World Bank GovTech work treats whole-of-government modernization, citizen-centered services, digital capabilities, and enabling institutions as connected maturity dimensions. [S23]

**[RESEARCH FINDING]** ILO’s GenAI work distinguishes occupational exposure from job loss and recommends managing transition with worker and institutional participation. [S24]

The practical lesson is that a private platform should offer a bounded service with defined accountability and interoperability. “AI labor intelligence” without provenance, representative coverage, institutional authority, or decision governance is not a credible public proposition.

## 6. User/Customer Jobs-to-be-Done

- **Policy owner:** understand whether a defined intervention addresses an established policy objective and how it will be evaluated.
- **Program operator:** enroll eligible participants, deliver service, track completion, manage providers, and produce auditable outcomes.
- **Platform operator:** protect reliability, identity, interoperability, and service boundaries.
- **Labor-market analyst:** obtain well-defined, representative or clearly non-representative evidence with reproducible methodology.
- **Education/quality authority:** improve transition evidence without confusing a vendor metric with official quality evidence.
- **Data/privacy authority:** ensure lawful, transparent, proportionate data use and rights.
- **Cybersecurity authority/customer:** understand scope, control maturity, incident management, and supply-chain risk.
- **Procurement/finance/legal:** buy a specified deliverable under a defensible contract, budget, and acceptance regime.
- **Citizen/beneficiary:** receive useful service without misleading authority claims, unfair exclusion, or uncontrolled data sharing.

## 7. Pain Points

1. Private vendors often use national-priority language without defining incremental contribution.
2. Pilots can create fragmented data and short-lived dashboards without operational ownership.
3. Program outputs can be counted as outcomes.
4. Self-selected platform data can be mistaken for population intelligence.
5. Memoranda can outpace delivery, data rights, or procurement.
6. Integration requests can precede security, legal, and architecture readiness.
7. Government-related sales cycles can distract a young company from proving core private utility.
8. Multiple mandates make “one government partner” an inaccurate concept.

## 8. Unmet Needs

- Private innovation that fits official architecture without pretending to be official.
- Bounded graduate/employer workflows whose evidence is auditable.
- Pilot methods with predefined population, outcomes, comparison limits, and exit conditions.
- Arabic-first career evidence and opportunity normalization.
- Clear separation between citizen-facing service data and policy/statistical use.
- Reliable employer closure and structured feedback evidence.
- Data-sharing agreements that specify purpose, field, frequency, retention, and publication.
- Institutional briefings that state what JID does not know and what it cannot access.

## 9. Market Structure

Government is not one segment. The market contains:

- sovereign strategy and policy bodies;
- regulators and standards authorities;
- program funders and operators;
- platform owners;
- statistical and observatory entities;
- education institutions and quality bodies;
- regional/sector development entities;
- public employers;
- procurement and shared digital-service functions.

Relationships progress through distinct states: awareness → listening → problem confirmation → technical/legal scoping → pilot authority → procurement/contract → delivery → evaluation → possible scale. A meeting skips none of these states.

## 10. Competitors/Substitutes

- Existing public platforms and programs, especially Jadarat and HRDF pathways.
- NLO/GASTAT official intelligence products.
- Government digital transformation vendors and consulting firms.
- University/employer direct systems and system integrators.
- Sector councils, training providers, and research organizations.
- Manual inter-agency processes where the cost of replacement exceeds the problem.

**[OPINION]** JID’s strongest public-sector alternative is often “no purchase; use existing systems.” The proposal must show a precise incremental capability and low integration risk.

## 11. Opportunities

Subject to formal authority and validation, JID could offer:

1. **Graduate transition pilot operations:** evidence completion, intervention routing, employer loop, and coverage-honest reporting.
2. **Employer vacancy/decision closure pilot:** freshness, response, closure reason, and structured communication—not a replacement job board.
3. **Program pathway discovery:** source-linked official opportunities such as Tamheer, without administering eligibility.
4. **Saudi skills crosswalk service:** map private evidence to official taxonomy terms where permitted.
5. **Regional/sector cohort service:** bounded participant and employer workflows with explicit selection bias.
6. **Research partnership:** privacy-preserving operational findings when methodology and publication rights are agreed.
7. **Accessibility and Arabic-first service evidence:** demonstrate inclusive transition workflows.

## 12. Risks

- Implied endorsement or misuse of official logos/names.
- Competition narrative against Jadarat or NLO.
- Unapproved access assumptions.
- Procurement dependency and slow receivables.
- Data-use expansion from service to policy analytics without authority.
- Re-identification in small cohorts or regions.
- Cybersecurity scope exceeding startup maturity.
- Unrepresentative data influencing policy.
- Public-sector customer concentration.
- Political/leadership transitions altering priorities.
- Press or investor claims preceding formal approval.
- Vendor lock-in concerns and unclear exit/data-return obligations.

## 13. What JID Should Do

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Adopt a public-institution engagement protocol:

1. name the institution’s published mandate;
2. state JID’s bounded, complementary proposition;
3. identify evidence and unknowns;
4. separate requested meeting, advice, data, pilot, procurement, and endorsement;
5. provide data, security, architecture, and claims summaries proportionate to stage;
6. record commitments and prohibited claims;
7. require founder and legal approval before any external commitment;
8. publish nothing about the relationship without written authorization.

Use private pilots to produce credible evidence while institutional dialogue checks alignment and terminology.

## 14. What JID Should Not Do

- Say “in partnership with” after a meeting or email.
- Use Vision 2030, ministry, authority, program, or official platform marks without permission.
- Pitch a Jadarat replacement or unofficial national labor observatory.
- imply access to government data, APIs, identity, or records.
- publish a national “skills gap,” “mismatch,” or “employability” percentage from JID data.
- make public-sector revenue a runway assumption before procurement evidence.
- accept a pilot without a named owner, outcome, data role, acceptance test, and exit plan.
- transfer citizen/graduate data between actors because it would be analytically useful.
- trade paid visibility for public-authority appearance.

## 15. Product/Service Implications

- Official-source records must show publisher, URL, publication date, retrieval date, freshness, scope, and terms.
- Institution-specific configurations must not change the meaning of shared metrics invisibly.
- Public-sector pilots need tenant isolation, export/return, audit, retention, and deletion controls.
- Every recommendation or insight should disclose source population and limitations.
- “Government program” and “government opportunity” are source classifications, not endorsements.
- Public-sector modules should reuse the core Career Record, Opportunity Graph, employer closure, and university intervention primitives; they should not become a separate product universe.

## 16. Data Implications

Classify data into:

- official public data and published reference material;
- licensed or agreement-governed institutional data;
- JID operational data;
- personal data and sensitive/high-risk subsets;
- aggregate/statistical outputs;
- derived features, recommendations, and models.

For each exchange define controller/processor roles, authority, exact fields, purpose, lawful basis, population, frequency, quality responsibility, access, localization/transfer, retention, security, onward use, publication, correction, incident response, and termination. “Anonymized” is not accepted without re-identification analysis.

## 17. Technology Implications Where Relevant

- Implement an institutional source registry and mandate-aware connector inventory.
- No production API or scraping integration before written rights, technical specification, and owner approval.
- Align government-facing architecture evidence to DGA/NORA where applicable, without claiming approval. [S13]
- Map cybersecurity controls to actual NCA scope and contract obligations. [S14]
- Maintain bilingual semantic/version control for occupations, sectors, programs, and regions.
- Ensure full auditability of import, transformation, access, recommendation, export, and deletion.
- Design for data portability and pilot exit; public value cannot depend on vendor hostage conditions.

## 18. Privacy/Legal Implications Where Relevant

PDPL applies to personal-data processing according to its scope; government interaction does not remove individual rights or JID accountability. [S10–S12]

Key gates include:

- formal legal basis and institutional authority;
- data-processing and sharing agreements;
- controller/processor allocation;
- data minimization and purpose boundaries;
- impact assessment where high risk;
- cross-border/subprocessor review;
- retention, return, deletion, and evidence of completion;
- security and incident notification terms;
- publication and statistical disclosure control;
- accessibility, consumer/user communication, and grievance paths;
- procurement, anti-bribery, conflicts, gifts/hospitality, and representation controls.

No role report is legal advice; actual agreements require Saudi counsel.

## 19. Business Model

Permissible models, only after procurement validation:

- fixed-fee diagnostic or research deliverable;
- fixed-scope pilot with acceptance milestones;
- program operations/service contract;
- annual workflow/platform license with implementation and support;
- data integration or reporting service under formal agreement;
- consortium/partner subcontract where roles are transparent.

Do not monetize personal data, influence, introductions, or implied endorsement. Public-sector margin must include procurement cost, security/legal burden, support, reporting, acceptance risk, and delayed cash—not only software gross margin.

## 20. Partnerships

Priority relationship categories:

1. MHRSD/HRDF/Jadarat for employment-platform and program context.
2. NLO/GASTAT for methodology and official-source boundaries.
3. MOE/ETEC and universities for graduate-transition and quality terminology.
4. SDAIA for PDPL guidance and data-governance interpretation channels.
5. DGA/NCA for applicable digital-government and cybersecurity expectations.
6. Sector councils/employers for demand evidence.
7. Regional institutions where a bounded cohort need is confirmed.

No category implies access, acceptance, or relationship today.

## 21. Validation Needed

- Which institution owns the problem?
- Is the need already solved or mandated elsewhere?
- Is JID a vendor, research partner, operator, processor, controller, or subprocessor?
- What is the procurement route, budget, timeline, and acceptance authority?
- What data are legally and technically available?
- What security/hosting/classification requirements apply?
- What constitutes public value and how is it evaluated?
- Can the pilot work without privileged integration?
- What happens to data and service when the pilot ends?
- What claims may JID make publicly?

The decisive validation is a scoped, budgeted path—not enthusiasm in a meeting.

## 22. 90-Day Actions

### Days 1–20

- Build the mandate/stakeholder map and relationship register.
- Create claims, logo, meeting-note, gift/conflict, and external-commitment protocols.
- Prepare a two-page Arabic/English institutional brief with explicit non-claims.
- Create a government data/partnership readiness checklist with GRC/CLO/CTO.

### Days 21–50

- Hold 3–5 authorized listening conversations across employment, outcomes/methodology, and education-transition categories.
- Ask about overlap, data authority, procurement, security, and evaluation; do not pitch assumed integrations.
- Run private actor pilots/discovery in parallel.

### Days 51–75

- Select at most one public-interest use case whose owner and boundary are clear.
- Draft a concept note with population, service, evidence, risk, cost, exit, and no-access fallback.
- Obtain legal, data, security, operations, and finance review.

### Days 76–90

- Submit founder options: continue dialogue, pursue a formal pilot path, or defer.
- Record every institution-specific gap and commitment.
- Exclude uncontracted public revenue and data from forecasts and architecture dependencies.

## 23. 12-Month Roadmap

- **Q1:** institutional map, listening, claims governance, readiness pack.
- **Q2:** one concept note only if private evidence supports it; begin procurement/security discovery.
- **Q3:** contract or formally defer; never keep an indefinite “strategic partnership” in the plan.
- **Q4:** deliver one bounded pilot or publish an internal no-go learning report. Scale only after evaluation and payment/authority evidence.

## 24. 3-Year Direction

**[HYPOTHESIS]** JID could become a trusted private infrastructure partner for bounded education-to-work and employer-workflow programs. The three-year path is: prove private utility → demonstrate governable evidence → deliver one public-interest pilot → standardize the service → expand only where mandates and economics fit.

JID should remain viable without government data or contracts. Institutional partnership should accelerate verified value, not be the source of product legitimacy.

## 25. Contribution to Saudi Leadership

Potential contribution includes:

- source-traceable career and opportunity evidence;
- better closure and beneficiary communication;
- operational support for graduate transition and employer feedback;
- alignment with Saudi occupation/skills language;
- privacy-preserving, coverage-honest pilot evidence;
- Arabic-first and region-aware delivery.

**[FACT]** These are proposed contributions. No official institution has adopted or endorsed them in the evidence reviewed.

## 26. Contribution to Global Potential

The global export is a model for private career infrastructure that cooperates with national systems rather than extracting or displacing them. A credible Saudi case could show how individual-controlled evidence, employer accountability, education interventions, and formal public boundaries coexist. The model will fail globally if it depends on informal access or national-brand claims.

## 27. Disagreements with Other Strategy Office Roles

1. **With CEO/Chair positioning:** “Career Evidence & Opportunity Operating System for Saudi Arabia” may sound like national infrastructure in government settings. Use “private complementary transition platform” until authority and scale are proven.
2. **With CMO:** Vision 2030 alignment must not become visual co-branding, endorsement language, or impact attribution.
3. **With CFO:** Government revenue should be zero in the base case until a budgeted procurement path exists; pipeline probability cannot be based on meetings.
4. **With CTO/CDO:** No official-source scraping, reverse engineering, or national intelligence layer should be architecture-critical before rights and methodology are settled.
5. **With Universities Director:** I support a private university pilot without prior government approval, but official terminology and non-duplication should be tested through early dialogue.
6. **With COO:** A government pilot must have an exit and data-return plan even if that reduces short-term contract attractiveness.

2. Authorize or prohibit institutional listening outreach during the 90-day validation period.
3. Name the founder or executive authorized to represent JID externally.

## 29. Research & Learning Appendix

### 29.1 Relationship-state register

Every institution is tracked as one state only:

1. Public-source mapped.
2. Intro requested.
3. Listening meeting held.
4. Problem owner confirmed.
5. Concept invited.
6. Technical/legal/procurement scoping.
7. Formally authorized pilot/procurement.
8. Contracted.
9. Delivering.
10. Evaluated/closed.

No lower state is described publicly as a partnership.

### 29.2 Public concept-note minimum

- published mandate and named owner;
- beneficiary and eligible population;
- problem and current substitute;
- JID service and explicit exclusions;
- evidence and hypothesis;
- measures with denominator/coverage;
- data role, fields, legal basis, security, retention;
- integration/no-integration option;
- accessibility and grievance route;
- cost, procurement, milestones, acceptance;
- exit, deletion/return, portability;
- public communications rights.

### 29.3 Stop conditions

Stop or redesign when: the use case duplicates an official platform without incremental value; data authority is absent; the requested claim is misleading; security scope exceeds feasible control maturity; the pilot cannot function without uncommitted access; individual rights cannot be respected; or economics require subsidy without strategic learning.
