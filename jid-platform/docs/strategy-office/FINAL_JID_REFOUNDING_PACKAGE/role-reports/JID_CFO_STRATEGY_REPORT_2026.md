# JID CFO Strategy Report 2026

**Role:** Chief Financial Officer
**Decision date:** 2026-08-25
**Evidence convention:** FACT = directly supported; RESEARCH FINDING = published analysis; MARKET SIGNAL = directional observation; HYPOTHESIS = requires validation; OPINION = role judgment; RECOMMENDATION = proposed action.

## 1. Executive Summary

**RECOMMENDATION:** Finance a 90-day evidence wedge, not a platform-wide rebuild. Preserve trustworthy technical assets, fund Career Record + Opportunity Graph + Radar + bounded hiring, and require commercial and usage gates before each expansion. Use a hybrid subscription/implementation model; prohibit business models that monetize hidden ranking, personal-data sale, or employment promises.

## 2. CFO Mandate

Translate strategy into capital allocation, economic hypotheses, pricing boundaries, unit-economics instrumentation, scenario controls, and stop/go gates. No forecast is a fact until supported by cohorts, contracts, or observable cost data.

## 3. What I Learned

**FACT:** The source package contains product breadth but no audited demand, conversion, price, retention, or support-cost dataset sufficient for a credible revenue forecast. **OPINION:** This is an uncertainty-management problem before it is a valuation story.

## 4. Saudi Current State

Saudi employment and human-capability infrastructure creates both opportunity and substitution risk. **HYPOTHESIS:** Employers and universities may pay for trusted coordination, workflow, and outcome evidence that complements public services; this must be tested with budget owners, not inferred from policy alignment.

## 5. Global Benchmark

Global platforms monetize recruiter workflow, institutional access, assessment usage, premium individual tools, implementation, and data products. **RECOMMENDATION:** Copy neither their scale economics nor their surveillance incentives; select revenue tied to delivered workflow value.

## 6. Jobs to Be Done

Finance must answer: Which actor pays? For what completed job? At what frequency? With which variable cost? Under what evidence of retention? What liability or service obligation accompanies the revenue?

## 7. Pain Points

Founder-led scope inflation, unclear SKU boundaries, expensive integrations, AI inference cost, high-touch enterprise onboarding, moderation burden, low-frequency individual use, and sales cycles that can mask weak product demand.

## 8. Unmet Needs

A costed product architecture; product-level P&L; event-defined activation and retention; implementation policy; pricing research; contract templates; AI cost caps; and a decision ledger linking spend to evidence.

## 9. Market Structure

The three external actors have different buyers and cycles. Individual revenue is high-volume/low-ARPU and trust-sensitive; Business is workflow-led with procurement and support; University is annual and implementation-heavy. Government work, if any, is contracted and should not subsidize an unfocused consumer product.

## 10. Competitors and Substitutes

Spreadsheets, email, public platforms, existing ATSs, career-center tools, and manual services set the effective price ceiling. Competitor list prices are not evidence of JID willingness to pay.

## 11. Opportunities

Employer design-partner subscriptions; paid implementation; university outcomes workspace; opt-in advanced Individual guidance; usage-based premium AI within caps; later verification services where evidence and liability are defined.

## 12. Risks

Building before willingness-to-pay; calling pilots revenue; unlimited AI usage; custom enterprise work without reuse; outcome guarantees; data-sale incentives; marketplace subsidies; and premature regional expansion.

## 13. What JID Should Do

Set a fixed 90-day envelope, attribute costs by product, meter AI, charge or secure signed pilot value where possible, and define conversion/retention/implementation gates before Phase 2.

## 14. What JID Should Not Do

Do not publish a three-year revenue number as a forecast. Do not promise free forever beyond the canonical Individual core. Do not accept bespoke work that fractures the product. Do not fund a broad feed, proprietary psychometric science, mass auto-apply, or full ATS replacement in the wedge.

## 15. Product and Service Implications

Entitlements must map to value units: workspace seats, active requisitions, managed cohorts, advanced recurring guidance, or implementation milestones. Each SKU needs service boundaries and cancellation/export rules.

## 16. Data Implications

Instrument acquisition cost, activation, cohort retention, opportunity actions, employer workflow completion, university cohort coverage, support hours, inference cost, gross margin, expansion, churn, and refunds. Separate contracted value, invoiced value, collected cash, and recognized revenue.

## 17. Technology Implications

Use existing Supabase/Postgres and CI assets before adding infrastructure. Apply per-tenant quotas, model routing, caching where safe, audit logs, and feature flags. Every new dependency needs total-cost and exit analysis.

## 18. Privacy and Legal Implications

PDPL, security, assessment validity, moderation, and retention obligations are costs and design constraints, not post-launch paperwork. Contractual liability must be bounded for recommendations, screening, data sources, and outcome reports.

## 19. Business Model

- Individual: canonical record, core discovery, truthful CV, and basic tracking free; paid advanced recurring guidance only.
- Business: subscription by workspace/requisition band, usage where value scales, and separate implementation.
- University: annual workspace/cohort band plus implementation.
- Government: explicit contracts only; no presumed grant or endorsement.

## 20. Partnerships

Partner economics must specify contribution, data rights, support burden, exclusivity, referral terms, and termination/export. Logo-only partnerships have no modeled value.

## 21. Validation Plan

Run Van Westendorp or structured price interviews only after problem validation; obtain signed design-partner agreements; observe support and integration hours; test paid continuation at pilot end; model base/downside/upside without presenting scenarios as forecasts.

## 22. First 90 Days

Create the cost baseline and hypothesis P&L; cap inference and contractor spend; price employer/university pilots; track weekly evidence gates; end with a continuation decision based on activation, retained use, partner willingness, gross-margin path, and risk closure.

## 23. Twelve-Month Direction

Seek repeatable employer and university packages, a controlled Individual paid tier, standard implementation, predictable support ratios, and one reliable acquisition channel per paying actor.

## 24. Three-Year Direction

Aim for a diversified, trust-compatible revenue base with no single customer or public contract controlling roadmap direction. Regional expansion follows Saudi unit economics and operating controls, not headline opportunity.

## 25. Saudi Leadership Path

Build Saudi financial durability by solving local transition problems, contracting transparently, using local compliance as design quality, and publishing honest methodology. Do not equate policy alignment with guaranteed demand.

## 26. Global Leadership Path

Portable evidence and interoperability can create global optionality with relatively low marginal product cost—only after localization, legal, tax, support, and partner economics are proven market by market.

## 27. Disagreements and Dissent

I dissent from launching five products as five commercial lines. They are one system but must be financed as a narrow wedge. I dissent from free high-cost generative use without caps and from pricing based on competitor benchmarks before JID-specific willingness-to-pay evidence.

## 28. Founder Decisions Required

Approve the 90-day capital envelope; free-core boundary; pilot pricing authority; AI usage caps; no-data-sale/no-pay-to-rank covenant; criteria for government contracts; and the exact gates that release Phase 2 funding.

## 29. Research Appendix

Required evidence: actual engineering capacity, infrastructure baseline, support burden, sales-cycle length, procurement requirements, design-partner budgets, price sensitivity, Individual retention frequency, model cost per completed job, and liability/insurance implications.
