# JID COO Strategy Report 2026

**Role:** Chief Operating Officer
**Study date:** 2026-08-25
**Status:** Independent operating recommendation; not adopted; no implementation, production, database, deployment, staffing, or contracting authority
**Evidence labels:** `FACT`, `RESEARCH FINDING`, `MARKET SIGNAL`, `HYPOTHESIS`, `OPINION`, `RECOMMENDATION`, `EVIDENCE GAP`

## 1. Executive Summary

**[RECOMMENDATION]** JID should operate the first 90 days as a **controlled learning service**, not as a feature launch. The operating unit is one closed loop: an Individual creates/reuses evidence, encounters a source-traceable opportunity, saves/prepares/acts, a verified employer advances or closes the workflow, and the result updates the record. A university readiness exercise runs in parallel but does not become a broad analytics launch.

The first operating model needs five named queues:

1. actor/organization onboarding and authority;
2. opportunity source/review/freshness;
3. employer hiring workflow and candidate communication;
4. trust, privacy, data-quality, safety and appeals;
5. partner support, evidence review and pilot conversion.

Every queue needs an owner, entry criteria, priority, service target, escalation, audit, correction, capacity, and closure state. Manual operations are acceptable when they reveal the real workflow, but they must be visible, measured, and designed for eventual productization. A spreadsheet, inbox, or staff workaround cannot silently become the system of record.

**[FACT]** Current repository reports demonstrate non-production controls and partial shipped capability, including a governed Lammah source/review pipeline. They do not prove production readiness, support capacity, source coverage, employer adoption, university readiness, or reliable incident response. [S03–S05]

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Use a hybrid rebuild operationally as well as technically: preserve identity/RLS/audit, Catalog/Lammah trust controls, bilingual/test foundations and institutional learning; replace actor journeys and workflows behind controlled flags. No big-bang migration, broad public launch, or clean-slate operational reset.

The COO’s success measure in 90 days is not registered users, pages shipped, jobs scraped, partners announced, or dashboards created. It is whether JID can run the loop repeatedly with acceptable user effort, source quality, employer closure, trust incidents, cost-to-serve, and partner commitment—and can stop safely if it cannot.

## 2. COO Mandate

The COO must turn strategy into a repeatable operating system: roles, procedures, queues, service levels, partner readiness, training, evidence capture, incident response, change control, quality reviews, capacity, and economics. The role determines what must be handled by people before automation and what cannot proceed without readiness.

The COO does not declare product-market fit, production readiness, legal compliance, model validity, or national impact. It ensures that every such claim has operating evidence and an accountable owner.

## 3. What I Learned

**[FACT]** JID’s current artifacts show significant delivery effort and governance controls but also release defects and a gap between shipped capability and actor validation. Code completion is not operational readiness. [S03]

**[FACT]** Lammah’s evidence, candidate, review, run, dead-letter, freshness, unsafe URL, entitlement, precedence, and kill-switch model is a strong template for operations: external input becomes publishable only through controlled states. [S04]

**[FACT]** Old Abhathli artifacts were deliberately removed. Any new search-for-me service requires a fresh operating mandate, source-rights review, assistance boundaries, support, and incident playbook. [S05]

**[RESEARCH FINDING]** The reviewed job-search reference emphasizes user review and warns that untrusted job text plus personal data is dangerous; instruction-level defenses are not a sandbox. Operations must assume tools will fail and content will be hostile. [S13]

**[RESEARCH FINDING]** Universities need explicit eligible populations, collection protocols, knowledge/coverage rates, and bias warnings. The operational work of contactability, consent, follow-up, verification and corrections comes before analytics. [S10–S11]

**[OPINION]** JID’s early competitive advantage can be operational integrity: fresh opportunities, respectful closure, reliable corrections, visible status, and fewer broken handoffs. Those are less glamorous than AI features and more likely to earn repeat trust.

## 5. Global Benchmark

- **LinkedIn and Handshake** show always-on multi-sided operations: identity, employer support, institutional relationships, content/moderation, opportunity quality and data systems. JID cannot imitate the surfaces without funding the operations. [S10, S12]
- **HESA/NACE** show that graduate outcomes are a collection operation and methodology, not merely reporting software. [S10–S11]
- **MadsLorentzen/ai-job-search** shows an end-to-end personal workflow with explicit review. Its lesson is stateful progression and truthfulness, not autonomous scale. [S13]
- **Open-source ATS/workflow/community platforms** show mature queues and states, but reviewed licenses and architectures make wholesale adoption risky; importing software would also import operational burdens. [S14]
- **Temporal/Trigger.dev/Hatchet versus Postgres queues** show an escalation path: start with one understandable queue/state model; add durable orchestration only when named failure modes require it. [S14]

## 6. Jobs to Be Done

### Individual operating jobs

- onboard without confusion;
- receive help correcting/importing evidence;
- trust that opportunities are fresh and application paths work;
- know what JID did and did not do;
- get respectful status/closure and escalate errors;
- exercise privacy rights and correct data.

### Employer operating jobs

- verify authority and organization;
- configure a repeatable hiring workflow;
- publish/import roles accurately;
- review candidates consistently;
- coordinate assessments/interviews;
- communicate and close every candidate;
- receive support without handing over uncontrolled data.

### University operating jobs

- qualify a cohort and responsible owners;
- establish contact/consent/lawful process;
- run collection and follow-up;
- reconcile sources and corrections;
- interpret coverage honestly;
- turn findings into one named action.

### Internal operating jobs

- know what work exists, who owns it, what is late, what failed, what harmed trust, and whether to continue.

## 7. Pain Points

Multi-actor workflows fail at handoffs. A recruiter does not close a stage; a source leaves an expired job live; an apply URL redirects; a user uploads inconsistent data; a university cannot contact graduates; an assessment vendor delays results; an SMS fails; a correction does not propagate; a staff member resolves a problem in chat without recording it.

The strategic program itself risks too many simultaneous surfaces—Career Record, Abhathli, Lammah, employer workflow, assessments, mentors, knowledge/social, university outcomes, government services. Without an operating sequence, each creates its own onboarding, support, moderation, quality, incident, and partner work.

Manual work is currently both necessary and dangerous. It can reveal the product, but if unbounded it conceals cost, inconsistent treatment, privacy exposure, and unsustainable service.

## 8. Unmet Needs

1. One pilot operating handbook with queues, roles, RACI, service targets and stop conditions.
2. A source operations desk for authorization, review, freshness, errors and kill switches.
3. Employer closure standards and candidate communication templates.
4. Career Record correction/evidence support with escalation.
5. Trust operations spanning privacy rights, safety, fraud, content, data quality, appeals and incidents.
6. Partner onboarding/certification before real data.
7. University readiness and collection operations before dashboards.
8. Capacity and cost-to-serve measurement by workflow.
9. Change management so product releases update SOPs, training and support.
10. A clear transition from manual learning to product automation or service pricing.

## 9. Market Structure

JID’s operating model has three service layers:

### Layer 1 — Self-service product

Canonical Career Record, CV projections, core discovery, Radar, basic employer workflow and privacy controls should become reliable product flows.

### Layer 2 — Managed operations

Source onboarding/review, organization verification, employer pilot setup, complex corrections, incident handling, assessment/vendor coordination and university collection require staff operations initially.

### Layer 3 — Professional/partner services

Employer program design, university readiness/methodology, integrations, and public-sector projects can remain paid services when standardized enough to price and govern.

**[OPINION]** JID should not hide Layer 2 or 3 inside SaaS pricing. A truthful operating model makes services visible, measures their cost, and decides which work to automate, standardize, partner, or stop.

## 10. Competitors and Substitutes

- Job platforms and Jadarat for discovery/application operations. [S06]
- Employer ATSs, agencies, recruitment-process outsourcers, spreadsheets and email for hiring operations.
- University career centers, alumni offices, institutional research and survey vendors for outcome collection.
- Calendar/video, assessment, communication and identity providers for specialist execution.
- LinkedIn/Handshake for multi-sided support and distribution. [S10, S12]
- WhatsApp and informal human coordinators for fast exception handling.

JID must beat informal substitutes on traceability and closure without becoming slower than them. It must complement authoritative partner systems until replacement is justified.

## 11. Opportunities

- Make fresh, source-traceable opportunities an operational promise with measurable review and correction.
- Differentiate through candidate closure: every managed application reaches a valid state or an escalated exception.
- Turn Career Record correction and evidence support into trust-building moments.
- Offer employer design partners a managed workflow that reveals what should become product.
- Sell university readiness and collection operations before analytics subscriptions.
- Standardize assessment and interview coordination while partnering for instruments/infrastructure.
- Create a bilingual trust/support function that informs product and data decisions.
- Use operational incidents and exception patterns as a disciplined learning loop.

## 12. Risks

- **Capacity risk:** pilots exceed staff ability and response times collapse.
- **Inconsistency risk:** manual reviewers treat similar cases differently.
- **Privacy risk:** staff see or export more personal data than needed.
- **Fraud risk:** fake organizations, credentials, opportunities, mentors, reviews or outcomes.
- **Source risk:** stale, unauthorized, duplicate or malicious opportunity content.
- **Automation risk:** retries, agents or integrations repeat actions or send wrong communications.
- **Vendor risk:** assessment, video, messaging, calendar or source provider outage.
- **Moderation risk:** professional/social experiments create abuse and appeals before staffing exists.
- **Reputation risk:** partnership or national claims overstate JID status.
- **Economics risk:** high-touch service is called scalable SaaS.
- **Change risk:** old and new workflows run simultaneously without reconciliation.
- **Key-person risk:** founder or one operator becomes the exception router.

## 13. What JID Should Do

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Establish a 90-day Pilot Operations Cell with one accountable pilot lead and named domain owners for Individual support, employer success, source/trust operations, data/privacy, and technical reliability. Roles may be combined at first, but accountabilities cannot be absent.

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Define queues in the product or an auditable staff system. Every manual action must reference the JID record, purpose, operator, evidence, result and next state.

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Run daily operational review during live pilot windows and weekly evidence/capacity/trust reviews. Use no-blame incident analysis and explicit corrective owners.

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Require readiness certification for every partner, source and vendor before real data: contract/authority, data map, security, test cases, owners, support, incident contact, rollback, exit and communication.

## 14. What JID Should Not Do

- Launch all actor journeys or a public social layer at once.
- Count provisioned accounts, imported jobs, messages, or partner logos as value.
- promise 24/7 support or enterprise SLA without staffing and evidence.
- allow staff to use personal messaging/files for sensitive case handling.
- publish unreviewed external opportunities or reactivate sources after failures without documented approval.
- let an AI agent submit, reject, offer, disclose, or communicate externally without deterministic authority and human control.
- use university percentages before cohort/coverage readiness.
- build proprietary assessments before content/validity/operations ownership exists.
- preserve manual steps indefinitely without cost, error and productization review.
- keep pilots alive because closure feels politically difficult.

## 15. Product and Service Implications

Operational requirements should shape product states:

- every onboarding flow can pause, resume, correct and escalate;
- opportunity records display freshness and can be quarantined/expired rapidly;
- Radar/application states include user-visible closure and exception states;
- employer workflow requires decision ownership and communication prompts;
- assessments include accommodation, consent, launch, completion, failure, result, review and appeal states;
- mentor/knowledge/social experiments include reporting, moderation, correction and appeal before exposure;
- university instruments include eligible, contactable, consented, responded, known/unknown, verified and corrected states;
- staff tools expose queues, priority, SLA, reason, history and kill switch.

Services should be documented product packages: employer design program, source onboarding, university readiness/collection, and integration certification.

## 16. Data Implications

Operations need minimum data, not broad access. Role-based staff projections should expose only fields required for the queue. Bulk exports are exceptional and audited.

Operational records include:

- queue entry, priority, assigned owner, due time and status;
- source/partner and authority version;
- contact attempts and channel result;
- review reasons and evidence;
- correction/appeal/incident linkage;
- manual effort and automation contribution;
- customer/partner communication and acknowledgement;
- final resolution and recurrence category.

Operational metrics need denominators and definitions. For example, “closure rate” must specify eligible managed applications and time window; “freshness” must specify source class and verification rule; “resolution time” must exclude or separately report user/partner waiting. Missing status is not rejection or unemployment.

## 17. Technology Implications

Use one queue abstraction and explicit state machines. The OSS review recommends evaluating PGMQ/Supabase Queues first, with pg-boss or Graphile Worker as alternatives—not mixing engines. [S14]

Operational technology requires:

- idempotent jobs/actions;
- retry/backoff/dead-letter and replay controls;
- dashboards for queue lag, failures, source health, notification delivery and SLA risk;
- staff authorization and audit;
- partner sandbox/test data;
- source and vendor kill switches;
- immutable evidence for consequential transitions;
- feature flags and rollback;
- bilingual templates with version approval;
- incident and change links to affected records.

Defer Temporal/Hatchet/Trigger.dev until multi-day signals/compensation/replay exceed queue plus state-machine needs. Use managed specialist providers rather than self-hosting complex video, proctoring or code execution during the first 90 days. [S14]

## 18. Privacy and Legal Implications

PDPL must be operationalized through intake and response procedures, identity verification for rights requests, correction/deletion propagation, retention jobs, disclosure logs, incident triage, vendor escalation, and evidence of completion. [S09]

Staff procedures require:

- least-privilege roles and regular review;
- approved channels/devices and no local uncontrolled copies;
- case notes that avoid unnecessary sensitive data;
- dual review for high-risk disclosure, merge, assessment, and institutional export;
- privacy/security incident severity and escalation;
- cross-border/vendor outage procedures;
- recording/proctoring consent and retention where later authorized;
- candidate appeal and correction routes;
- legal approval for employment-affecting automation and partner terms.

The COO cannot substitute an SOP for lawful basis or counsel. It ensures the chosen legal/privacy rule is actually followed and measurable.

## 19. Business Model

Operations must reveal real unit economics:

### Employer

Track onboarding hours, opportunity setup, recruiter support, candidate volume, communications, integration work, incident/support load and renewal effort. Price design programs and enterprise service honestly; do not bury implementation cost.

### University

Track cohort setup, contactability, instrument configuration, outreach, follow-up, reconciliation, verification, analysis and stakeholder workshops. Readiness and collection are billable value before dashboards.

### Individual

Core support for rights, corrections, factual record and platform errors should not require premium payment. Premium preparation/mentor services need separate service standards and refunds/disputes.

### Vendors

Attribute per-use and fixed costs by workflow. A managed vendor may be cheaper than internal operations even with higher unit price; self-host only after total cost and control justify it.

## 20. Partnerships

Partner operations need named counterparts for executive sponsorship, daily workflow, data/privacy, security/IT, procurement, and incident response. One champion is insufficient.

### Readiness checklist

- defined cohort/workflow and success/stop conditions;
- signed scope or approved discovery charter;
- authority and data schedule;
- source rights/license/terms;
- user communication and consent where applicable;
- environments, credentials and test cases;
- operating hours, SLA and escalation tree;
- incident, correction, deletion and exit procedures;
- training and acceptance;
- conversion/closure review date;
- exact public-claim permission.

Prefer three to five employer partners with repeated early-career demand and one university readiness partner. More partners increase learning only if the Operations Cell can serve them consistently.

## 21. Validation Plan

### Individual loop evidence

- onboarding completion and assisted-step rate;
- evidence correction/import effort;
- repeat Career Record use;
- opportunity freshness and broken-link incidents;
- save-to-preparation/action progression;
- support, privacy and trust incidents;
- user-reported clarity and control.

### Employer loop evidence

- time to activate employer/operator;
- roles managed and weekly recruiter use;
- stages closed with reason/communication;
- median workflow waiting by owner;
- manual effort per candidate/role;
- candidate complaints/appeals;
- willingness to continue/pay.

### Source evidence

- authorization; successful runs; review yield; duplicate/error/broken URL; freshness/expiry; correction latency; dead letters; kill-switch events.

### University readiness evidence

- eligible population, contactability, lawful process, response/known outcome, missingness, small-cell risk, staff action and cost.

### Operating falsification

Stop or narrow if queues are not controllable, partner workflow is not repeated, closure is not improved, manual cost is disproportionate, source rights/quality fail, trust incidents are unacceptable, or users do not understand authority.

## 22. First 90 Days

### Days 0–15: establish control

- Appoint the Pilot Operations Cell and RACI.
- Freeze the pilot inclusion/exclusion list.
- Define queue taxonomy, service targets, escalation, incident severity and daily/weekly cadence.
- Inventory current support, source, partner, privacy and data-quality work.
- Select candidate employers/sources/university based on operational readiness.

### Days 16–30: rehearse

- Write SOPs and bilingual templates for onboarding, source review, opportunity correction, candidate communication, privacy rights, incident response, partner outage and pilot closure.
- Configure auditable non-production queues and synthetic scenarios.
- Train operators; run tabletop exercises for stale source, malicious posting, wrong disclosure, duplicate merge, vendor outage, delayed assessment and data-rights request.
- Establish baseline capacity and cost capture.

### Days 31–60: controlled operation

- Onboard partners sequentially, not all at once.
- Run one authorized source through Lammah controls.
- Support Career Record/Opportunity/Radar/employer closure thin slice under flags.
- Hold daily queue/incident review and weekly partner/evidence review.
- Log every manual intervention and recurrence category.

### Days 61–90: pilot and decision

- Run repeated cycles with bounded cohorts.
- Execute university readiness/collection rehearsal only if its prerequisites pass.
- Rehearse rollback, source kill, vendor failure, rights request and partner exit.
- Review utility, closure, source quality, trust, capacity and economics.
- Convert, extend once for a named gap, or close each pilot/capability.

## 24. Three-Year Direction

By year three, JID should have a mature operating model for verified actors, authorized opportunities, employer hiring closure, Career Record corrections, assessments/integrations, institutional outcomes, trust/moderation, and partner support.

Operations should be increasingly productized but retain human review for high-impact, ambiguous, fraud, privacy, appeals and institutional claims. Global expansion requires a local operating adapter for language, law, sources, programs, calendars, vendors and escalation—not only technical localization.

## 26. Global Leadership Path

The globally portable operating advantage is **trustworthy closure**. Most platforms optimize discovery or engagement; JID can distinguish itself by operating the full evidence-to-outcome journey with explicit states, source quality, corrections, rights, and human escalation.

Global scale should standardize the operating kernel—queues, RACI, service definitions, partner certification, data/claim controls, incident learning—while localizing sources, laws, language and institutions.

- **Business & Partnerships:** Three to five employers is a maximum only if capacity supports them. I will reject a prestigious partner with no daily owner, data authority or closure commitment.
- **CTO:** Manual steps should not be dismissed as technical debt during discovery. They are acceptable when audited and costed. I agree they must not become hidden architecture.
- **CDO:** Minimum definitions, source and purpose are mandatory; full ontology perfection is not. Operations needs provisional codes and a governed path to refine them.
- **CPO/UX:** The product may feel alive through useful state and response, not through launching a feed. Professional/social experiments wait for moderation capacity.
- **CFO:** Do not judge a discovery pilot by mature gross margin, but cap subsidy and record every hour/vendor cost.
- **CMO:** Response targets, source coverage and outcomes cannot be marketed until operating evidence supports the exact population and period.
- **GRC/CLO:** A control that cannot be executed under realistic queue load is not an effective control; join tabletop and pilot reviews.

### Proposed operating RACI

| Workflow | Accountable | Responsible | Consulted | Informed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pilot outcome | COO/pilot lead | Partner success + domain operators | CEO, product, CTO, CDO, GRC/legal | Founder/council |
| Source publication | Source/trust lead | Source reviewers | CTO/security, legal, product | Partner success |
| Employer closure | Employer success lead | Recruiter/operator | Product, trust, data | Candidate |
| Career Record correction | Individual operations lead | Support/data steward | Privacy, product | Individual |
| Privacy right/incident | Privacy/GRC owner | Trust operations | CLO, CTO/security, CDO | Executive/affected parties as required |
| University metric release | University/data owner | Data/research operations | Privacy/legal, university sponsor | Approved audience |

### Initial service targets for validation, not public SLA

Targets must be set after capacity rehearsal. The initial operating principle is risk-based priority:

- P0 security/privacy/safety: immediate acknowledgement and executive escalation;
- P1 wrong disclosure, fraudulent/stale active opportunity, broken critical application path: same operating day triage;
- P2 workflow blockage or partner failure: next operating-day triage;
- P3 content/data correction and general support: queued by published pilot expectation.

Exact hours are founder/operations decisions after staffing; none are claimed here.

### Manual-step graduation rule

Productize when the step is frequent, stable, measurable, low-ambiguity and worth the error/engineering trade-off. Keep human review when impact, ambiguity, fraud, legal judgment, appeals, or low volume dominate. Stop the step when actor value does not justify its cost or risk.

### Pilot exit review

For each capability/partner answer: Was the job repeated? Did the user/partner act? Was closure improved? What manual work and cost occurred? What failures/harm appeared? Are source/data rights durable? Will the partner pay/contribute? What should be automated, standardized, partnered or stopped?
