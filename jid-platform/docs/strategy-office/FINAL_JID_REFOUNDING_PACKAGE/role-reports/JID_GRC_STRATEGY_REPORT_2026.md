# JID GRC Strategy Report 2026

**Role:** Governance, Risk & Compliance Director
**Study date:** 2026-08-25
**Status:** Independent role recommendation; not founder-adopted; not a compliance certification or legal opinion
**Evidence labels:** `FACT`, `RESEARCH FINDING`, `MARKET SIGNAL`, `HYPOTHESIS`, `OPINION`, `RECOMMENDATION`

## 1. Executive Summary

**[RECOMMENDATION]** JID should make governed trust a product capability before it makes it a marketing claim. The company’s proposed value depends on persistent career evidence, recommendations, employer decisions, university reporting, external opportunity sources, and possible professional interaction. Each creates consequences for identity, privacy, fairness, security, fraud, content safety, institutional assurance, and business continuity.

The minimum governance system is not a policy folder. It is a set of enforced records and gates: processing inventory; data classification; purpose/legal-basis map; risk register; AI/use-case register; source-rights register; third-party register; control ownership; incident/complaint/rights workflows; claims approval; assessment-validation gate; and launch/exception decisions with residual-risk acceptance.

**[FACT]** Saudi PDPL establishes data-subject rights and controller duties around purpose, lawful processing, minimization, accuracy, retention, and accountability. [S01–S03] **[FACT]** NCA’s ECC 2:2024 provides a strong cybersecurity control benchmark for national entities in scope; private JID applicability is not established merely by its publication. [S04] **[FACT]** DGA policies apply in government contexts and become material when JID pursues public integration or contracts; they do not certify a private product. [S05]

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Prohibit, until separately approved: autonomous high-impact candidate rejection; universal employability/commitment/fit scores; mass auto-application; silent cross-actor disclosure; unlicensed opportunity ingestion; institution-level outcome percentages without coverage; and a general social launch without moderation, abuse, incident, and appeal readiness.

The proposed risk appetite is **low** for rights harm, misleading claims, security compromise, unfair employment effects, and unauthorized external action; **moderate and bounded** for reversible usability and commercial experiments using minimal data; and **zero tolerance** for fabricated evidence, covert data sale, bribery, retaliation, or knowing regulatory evasion.

## 2. Mandate

The GRC Director builds the system by which JID identifies obligations, decides risk, proves controls, handles failure, and learns. The role covers:

- corporate and product governance;
- privacy/data protection operations;
- cybersecurity governance and assurance;
- AI/model and automated-decision risk;
- assessment validity/fairness governance;
- third-party, source, and supply-chain risk;
- fraud, impersonation, content, and platform-abuse risk;
- business continuity, incident, complaint, and remediation;
- control evidence, audit, exceptions, and founder/board reporting.

GRC does not replace Saudi counsel, independent security testing, assessment science, the product owner, or management accountability.

## 3. What I Learned

**[FACT]** JID’s proposed system crosses three sensitive institutional contexts: an individual’s career identity, employer selection, and university outcome/service records. A datum can be harmless in one context and harmful when disclosed or inferred in another.

**[FACT]** Official Saudi sources already define Jadarat, national statistics, graduate outcomes, and skills structures. [S06–S10] Mislabeling JID data as official or representative is a governance failure even if the number is technically calculated correctly.

**[RESEARCH FINDING]** ILO distinguishes GenAI exposure from job loss, while OECD warns that skills-first markets require credible validation and governance. [S11–S12] WEF projections are employer expectations, not individual predictions. [S13]

**[RESEARCH FINDING]** NIST AI RMF and its Generative AI profile frame AI risk across governance, mapping, measurement, and management rather than treating a model call as a feature toggle. [S14–S15]

**[MARKET SIGNAL]** The internal package identifies ghost jobs, low-signal applications, social-performance harms, and employer mismatch. No reviewed primary source establishes their Saudi prevalence. GRC must stop these hypotheses becoming marketing or automated policy.

## 4. Saudi Current State

- **[FACT]** PDPL and its implementing/guidance materials establish rights and accountability relevant to JID’s persistent personal-data processing. [S01–S03]
- **[FACT]** NCA ECC 2:2024 supplies a current control framework with defined scope. [S04]
- **[FACT]** DGA digital-government policy and national enterprise architecture are relevant to government delivery/integration scope. [S05]
- **[FACT]** The Saudi E-Commerce Law regulates online service-provider disclosure, contracting, consumer data, and related electronic-commerce practices where in scope. [S16]
- **[FACT]** The Electronic Transactions Law supplies a legal framework for electronic records, transactions, and signatures. [S17]
- **[FACT]** MHRSD announced that amendments to the Labor Law took effect on 2025-02-19. Employer screening, assessment, offers, and workplace-related features require current employment-law review. [S18]

No single framework answers every product decision. JID needs an obligations register showing entity, product, user, transaction, data, jurisdiction, contract, and regulatory scope.

## 5. Global Benchmark

- **NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0:** Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover create an evidence-based cybersecurity operating model. [S19]
- **NIST AI RMF/GenAI Profile:** AI risk is use-case and lifecycle dependent; evaluation, human oversight, incident learning, and third-party risk matter. [S14–S15]
- **OECD AI Principles:** responsible stewardship emphasizes human-centered values, transparency, robustness, security, safety, and accountability. [S20]
- **ILO/OECD employment evidence:** exposure and skills change should not be converted into deterministic personal outcomes. [S11–S12]

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Use these as risk-management benchmarks, not badges. Certification or legal compliance claims require the appropriate independent process.

## 6. User/Customer Jobs-to-be-Done

- **Individual:** know what JID holds, why, who can see it, how a recommendation was produced, and how to correct, withdraw, appeal, or leave.
- **Employer:** trust identity and evidence states, run job-relevant selection, control access, prevent fraud, and demonstrate accountable decisions.
- **University:** use data within authority, understand coverage, limit access, correct errors, and produce honest aggregate reporting.
- **Founder/board:** decide risk appetite, see material exposure, accept residual risk explicitly, and fund controls.
- **Product/engineering/operations:** receive concrete control requirements and fast exception decisions.
- **Regulator/customer assurance:** obtain accurate scope, control evidence, incident handling, and remediation—not marketing assertions.

## 7. Pain Points

1. Consent is often treated as a universal solution instead of one legal basis within a purpose-controlled system.
2. Product teams can classify AI by technical method rather than consequence.
3. “Verified” can hide who verified what, how, and when.
4. Logging can become excessive surveillance; insufficient logging prevents accountability.
5. Institutional access can expand through convenience.
6. Metrics can omit denominator, coverage, or missingness.
7. Third-party tools and external sources can introduce security, privacy, IP, and continuity risk.
8. Social/professional features create moderation, harassment, impersonation, fraud, and child/young-person risks.
9. Control evidence often begins only when an enterprise asks for it.
10. A startup may accept hidden operational risk to preserve launch speed.

## 8. Unmet Needs

- Decision-linked evidence labels visible in product and reporting.
- Fine-grained disclosure and access purpose.
- A reliable rights/complaints/correction center.
- Assessment validity and adverse-impact review before employer use.
- Source and external-action authorization controls for Abhathli.
- Security and abuse readiness proportionate to professional identity value.
- University metric coverage and small-cell governance.
- Enterprise assurance pack based on real evidence.
- Reversible experimentation with predefined stop conditions.
- Founder-visible residual-risk decisions rather than informal exceptions.

## 9. Market Structure

Trust in JID is multi-party and asymmetric. The Individual bears privacy and opportunity risk; the employer bears hiring, fraud, and workflow risk; the university bears institutional, cohort, and reporting risk; JID bears platform, regulatory, reputational, security, and contractual risk.

Revenue incentives can conflict with trust: employer payment may pressure ranking; university payment may pressure favorable metrics; individual subscriptions may pressure anxiety; sponsored content may pressure reach; government opportunity may pressure claims. GRC must govern these conflicts, not merely list them.

## 10. Competitors/Substitutes

- PDPL/cybersecurity/legal advisers and independent assessors.
- enterprise governance, privacy, security, trust/safety, and GRC tooling.
- assessment publishers with validation and governance.
- customer vendor-assurance processes.
- internal spreadsheets, policy folders, and founder judgment.
- choosing not to process, infer, publish, integrate, or automate.

**[OPINION]** “Do not collect or automate” is often the safest substitute and must remain a real option.

## 11. Opportunities

1. Make evidence provenance and disclosure control a visible trust differentiator.
2. Offer employers structured, auditable workflows rather than black-box ranking.
3. Give universities methodology and coverage controls that reduce reporting risk.
4. Build Arabic-first rights, complaint, and explanation experiences.
5. Turn a maintained assurance pack into enterprise sales acceleration.
6. Use a source-rights registry to make opportunity discovery more durable.
7. Establish controlled professional interaction rather than engagement-at-any-cost.
8. Develop a reusable governance model for global career infrastructure.

## 12. Risks

### Critical risk themes

- Account takeover, identity fraud, forged credentials, employer impersonation.
- Unauthorized disclosure of career, education, assessment, salary, disability, or preference data.
- Discriminatory or invalid assessment/recommendation effects.
- AI hallucination, false fit, untrusted content, prompt injection, and silent external action.
- Scraping/source contractual violations and stale opportunities.
- Employer ghosting or vacancy misrepresentation being implied as solved when not measured.
- University outcome misrepresentation, small-cell disclosure, and re-identification.
- Harassment, spam, scams, defamatory content, fake expertise, and manipulation in professional interaction.
- Inadequate complaint, correction, appeal, or incident response.
- Third-party outage, model/provider change, or subprocessor drift.
- Ransomware, destructive error, failed backup/restore, and operational concentration.
- Misleading compliance, government, partnership, outcome, or AI claims.
- Paid visibility corrupting opportunity fairness.
- Youth/under-18 participation without an approved age/safeguarding model.
- Regulatory or contract scope growing beyond JID control maturity.

## 13. What JID Should Do

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Establish seven mandatory decision gates:

1. **Data gate:** purpose, legal basis, minimum fields, rights, retention, roles.
2. **Source gate:** ownership/terms/API, freshness, provenance, failure/exit.
3. **AI gate:** consequence tier, evaluation, human control, explanation, monitoring, stop rule.
4. **Assessment gate:** job relevance, validity, language/localization, bias, accommodation, scoring governance.
5. **Institution gate:** authority, contract, tenancy, coverage, publication, deletion/return.
6. **Interaction gate:** identity, moderation, reporting, appeals, abuse metrics, crisis response.
7. **Claims gate:** source, wording, population, period, uncertainty, approval, expiry.

Each gate ends `APPROVE`, `APPROVE_WITH_CONTROLS`, `PILOT_ONLY`, `DEFER`, or `PROHIBIT`, with owner and review date.

## 14. What JID Should Not Do

- sell, barter, or covertly repurpose personal data;
- call any score “employability,” “commitment,” “potential,” or “fit” without validated meaning and consequence controls;
- permit autonomous candidate rejection or mass auto-application in the initial system;
- hide a recommendation’s material inputs or make correction impossible;
- expose institutional cohort data below approved coverage/cell thresholds;
- launch paid mentors, public reviews, or a general social layer before transaction, moderation, fraud, and safeguarding controls exist;
- claim PDPL/NCA/DGA/ISO compliance or government approval without the correct assessment and scope;
- continue a product experiment after its harm stop condition is met;
- allow a commercial executive to accept critical residual rights/security risk alone.

## 15. Product/Service Implications

Trust must be visible at decision points:

- labels for self-declared, source-linked, issuer-confirmed, platform-observed, assessment-derived, and administratively verified evidence;
- “why shown” and “why recommended” explanations without fake percentages;
- access preview before disclosure;
- source/freshness and application destination for opportunities;
- report/correct/challenge/withdraw controls;
- clear employer identity and vacancy owner/status;
- university coverage and methodology panel before KPIs;
- finite professional interactions with rate, reach, reporting, and moderation controls;
- external-action confirmation and receipt for any Abhathli action.

## 16. Data Implications

### Classification

- **D0 Public:** approved public reference data.
- **D1 Internal:** non-public business/operational information.
- **D2 Personal:** identity, contact, career, behavioral, and account data.
- **D3 Restricted:** assessment results, compensation, precise constraints/location, institutional records, security data, legal/complaint records, and other high-consequence data.
- **D4 Prohibited by default:** data with no approved necessity/basis; covert surveillance; irrelevant sensitive traits; credentials/secrets in analytics; raw third-party data without rights.

Classification determines collection, encryption, access, logging, export, retention, test use, sharing, and deletion. Derived data inherits the highest relevant consequence until reviewed. “Aggregate” is not automatically non-personal.

## 17. Technology Implications Where Relevant

Minimum control objectives include:

- strong authentication and privileged-access management;
- tenant and actor isolation enforced server-side and at the database boundary;
- secrets/key management and encryption in transit/at rest;
- immutable-enough security/audit events with privacy minimization;
- secure SDLC, dependency/vulnerability management, change review, and environment separation;
- rate limiting, anti-automation, abuse/fraud signals, and account recovery;
- backup, restore testing, recovery objectives, and incident communications;
- data inventory, retention jobs, rights fulfillment, and export/deletion evidence;
- model/provider inventory, prompt/source isolation, evaluation, monitoring, and kill switch;
- subprocessor and source change monitoring;
- access recertification and control-evidence collection.

No control is assumed implemented because it appears in a design document; evidence must show operating effectiveness.

## 18. Privacy/Legal Implications Where Relevant

Privacy by design requires a processing record that states actor, data, source, purpose, legal basis, controller/processor role, recipients, retention, location/transfer, security, rights, and high-risk assessment. [S01–S03]

Additional legal/compliance scope includes e-commerce and consumer transactions, electronic records/signatures, employment/assessment use, IP/source rights, platform terms, advertising, mentor payments, university/public procurement, record retention, accessibility, and incident obligations. [S16–S18]

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Do not phrase counsel-dependent interpretation as settled fact. Maintain an advice register: question, facts presented, counsel, date, jurisdiction, conclusion, conditions, owner, review trigger.

## 19. Business Model

GRC is not a separate compliance product in the first three years. It enables revenue by reducing enterprise/university assurance friction, preventing remediation, and supporting trustworthy premium workflows.

Costs to fund explicitly:

- privacy/legal counsel and DPO capability;
- security testing, monitoring, backup/recovery, and incident response;
- assessment validation/localization;
- moderation/trust operations;
- vendor due diligence and contract review;
- audit/control evidence and insurance where appropriate.

Do not sell “verified,” “fair,” “secure,” or “compliant” tiers when baseline user rights and security should apply to all. Premium may fund additional workflow and service, not weaker protection for free users.

## 22. 90-Day Actions

### Days 1–20

- Appoint accountable risk, privacy, security, and product owners.
- Build obligations, processing, data, AI, source, vendor, and risk registers.
- Approve provisional risk appetite and prohibited-use list.
- Map launch gates to the 90-day product validation loop.

### Days 21–45

- Complete privacy/threat/abuse modelling for Career Record, Opportunity Graph, Radar, employer workspace, and university pilot.
- Run access-control, backup/restore, rights, and incident tabletop checks in the appropriate non-production environment.
- Create claims and evidence approval workflow.

### Days 46–70

- Review proposed external sources, model/providers, and pilot vendors.
- Define AI and assessment evaluation protocols.
- Prepare incident, breach, complaint, appeal, and external-communications playbooks.

### Days 71–90

- Issue a control-readiness report with evidence, gaps, residual risks, and owner/dates.
- Founder decides exceptions and high-risk deferrals.
- Provide enterprise/university assurance pack only with verified claims.

## 23. 12-Month Roadmap

- **Q1:** registers, appetite, gates, priority threat/privacy models, incident tabletop.
- **Q2:** first pilot controls, third-party program, rights metrics, restore/security tests.
- **Q3:** independent penetration/privacy/AI or assessment reviews proportionate to launched scope; social/mentor readiness decision.
- **Q4:** control operating-effectiveness review, business-continuity exercise, customer assurance package, annual residual-risk report.

## 24. 3-Year Direction

Year 1 establishes truth and control evidence. Year 2 adds scalable enterprise/university assurance, formal AI/assessment monitoring, and mature trust/safety operations if interaction launches. Year 3 can pursue appropriate certifications or formal customer/regulatory assurance where market evidence justifies the cost.

**[HYPOTHESIS]** The durable moat is not a certificate; it is a product and operating record that can show where evidence came from, who used it, what decision occurred, what control applied, and how errors were corrected.

Global professional platforms often centralize identity, attention, opportunity, and recruitment power. A governance architecture that travels with those primitives is globally valuable; a Saudi-only checklist is not.

## 27. Disagreements with Other Strategy Office Roles

1. **With CEO/CPO:** Calling AI “assistive” does not make it low risk. Consequence, data, reliance, and contestability determine the tier.
2. **With CMO:** No general professional feed or public mentor reviews should launch to create return frequency before moderation, fraud, crisis, and appeal readiness.
3. **With CFO:** Privacy, security, assessment validation, and incident capability are not optional overhead to defer until revenue; their cost belongs in unit economics.
4. **With CDO:** A unified intelligence lake is not the starting point. Purpose-specific records, minimization, provenance, and access boundaries come first.
5. **With CTO:** Technical feasibility and RLS tests do not settle lawful purpose, fairness, source rights, or operational control effectiveness.
6. **With CLO:** Legal permissibility is the floor. GRC may still recommend against a lawful practice because residual trust, fairness, concentration, or operational risk is unacceptable.
7. **With Universities/Government Relations:** Institutional prestige never justifies a pilot whose data roles, coverage, exit, or security are unresolved.

## 28. Decisions Required from Founder

1. Adopt, revise, or reject the proposed risk appetite and zero-tolerance statements.
2. Appoint accountable privacy, security, AI/assessment, trust/safety, and risk owners with escalation independence.
3. Prohibit or permit autonomous candidate rejection; **GRC recommends prohibition.**
4. Prohibit or permit mass auto-application; **GRC recommends prohibition.**
5. Decide the minimum age/safeguarding model before under-18 participation or paid mentoring.
6. Decide whether a social/professional layer is excluded from year one unless the interaction gate passes.
7. Approve the mandatory metric/claims gate and no-fake-score rule.
8. Approve a security/control baseline mapped to NIST CSF and, where relevant, NCA ECC—without claiming certification.
9. Define who may accept critical residual risk and the maximum exception duration.
10. Fund independent counsel/testing and an incident reserve.

### 29.2 Risk decision record

Every material decision records: objective; affected actors; evidence; alternatives; inherent risk; required controls; residual risk; owner; approver; expiry; monitoring; stop condition; and outcome. Silence is not acceptance.

### 29.3 Evidence labels in controls

- A `FACT` can still be out of scope or obsolete.
- A `RESEARCH FINDING` must retain population/method.
- A `MARKET SIGNAL` may justify research, not a high-impact rule.
- A `HYPOTHESIS` must have a falsification and stop condition.
- An `OPINION` cannot authorize data use.
- A `RECOMMENDATION` requires an accountable decision.
