# JID Founder Strategic Decision Memo

**Date:** 2026-08-25
**Purpose:** Decision-ready closeout of the strategic re-founding program
**Rule:** Recommendations are not approvals. Product implementation remains prohibited until the required decisions and task packets are authorized.

## Recommended decision bundle

Adopt JID provisionally as a Saudi career-evidence and opportunity operating system; retain exactly three actors; select a hybrid rebuild; fund a 90-day Career Record-led wedge; prohibit data sale, paid organic ranking, mass auto-apply, fabricated fit scores, emotion inference, and opaque automated rejection; defer a general social network; and explicitly decide whether the proposed Constitution supersedes the current binding repository Constitution.

## FD-01 — What category is JID?

- **A — Job/professional platform:** Familiar, but places JID into listing/feed competition and weakens the evidence thesis.
- **B — Career-evidence and opportunity operating system (recommended):** Connects the three actors through one evidence loop.
- **C — Institutional analytics vendor:** Easier enterprise framing, but abandons the Individual-controlled core.
- **Why B:** It best explains Career Record, opportunity intelligence, hiring, and outcomes as one system.
- **Reversibility/cost:** Medium; naming can change, architecture and market learning are costly.
- **Risk of delay:** Teams continue building unrelated products.
- **Evidence required:** Positioning tests and real task use; category language is still provisional.

## FD-02 — Which Constitution governs?

- **A — Keep current Constitution unchanged:** Safest operationally, but conflicts with the founder's re-founding intent and some reopened product questions.
- **B — Explicitly adopt the proposed Constitution through an article-level amendment (recommended):** Separates immutable trust/legal principles from current strategy and experiments.
- **C — Treat the Master Command as automatic supersession:** Fast but unsafe, ambiguous, and contrary to the repository amendment discipline.
- **Why B:** It resolves authority without pretending that a strategy report edits binding governance.
- **Reversibility/cost:** Constitutional changes are deliberately high-friction.
- **Risk of delay:** No coherent implementation authority; teams may cherry-pick conflicting documents.
- **Evidence required:** Founder decision plus legal/privacy/security and repository governance review.

## FD-03 — Public actor model

- **A — Three actors: Individual, Business, University (recommended).**
- **B — Add Government:** Confuses authority/partner/customer context with product identity and raises governance scope.
- **C — Add Mentor:** Creates a redundant actor; mentorship is an Individual capability.
- **Why A:** Preserves a proven boundary and still supports both Government relationships and mentors.
- **Reversibility/cost:** High if identity/RLS and IA are rebuilt later.
- **Risk of delay:** Product and permissions fragment.
- **Evidence required:** Amend only if a truly distinct rights/ownership model emerges.

## FD-04 — Build, rebuild, or preserve

- **A — Continue incremental feature addition:** Lowest immediate cost, retains current IA/domain debt.
- **B — Greenfield rewrite:** Maximum design freedom, discards trust and delivery assets.
- **C — Hybrid rebuild (recommended):** Preserve identity/RLS/audit, actor boundaries, Catalog, Lammah, i18n, CI/tests; rebuild IA and new domain contracts.
- **Why C:** Repository evidence supports reusable foundations but not the current product shape as the new strategy.
- **Reversibility/cost:** Medium; contract/IA decisions should be staged behind flags.
- **Risk of delay:** Sunk-cost debate replaces customer evidence.
- **Evidence required:** Code/domain mapping and migration/rollback packets before implementation.

## FD-05 — First 90-day wedge

- **A — Individual-only assistant:** Faster, but does not test the evidence return loop.
- **B — Institutional dashboard:** Easier demo, but risks denominator-free analytics and weak Individual agency.
- **C — Career Record + Opportunity Graph/Lammah + Abhathli/Radar + one employer loop (recommended).**
- **Why C:** Smallest end-to-end test of the system thesis.
- **Reversibility/cost:** High reversibility if controlled and flag-gated.
- **Risk of delay:** More research without operational evidence.
- **Evidence required:** Named segment, committed design partners, rights model, gate metrics.

## FD-06 — First segments

- **A — Recent Saudi graduates + graduate/early-career employers (provisional recommendation).**
- **B — Mid-career professionals + professional employers:** Potentially higher willingness to pay, more heterogeneous evidence.
- **C — University students broadly + all employers:** Larger surface, weak focus.
- **Why A:** Aligns naturally with University transition needs and bounded early-career evidence, but the source package does not prove it.
- **Reversibility/cost:** High reversibility before build.
- **Risk of delay:** Partner recruitment and research remain unfocused.
- **Evidence required:** 12–18 Individual studies, 3–5 employer design partners, and buyer workflow observation by Day 15.

## FD-07 — AI and automation boundary

- **A — Aggressive automation including application submission and ranking:** Strong demo effect, high trust/legal/source risk.
- **B — Assistive, explainable, human-authorized AI (recommended):** Retrieve, explain, prepare, draft, track; humans authorize consequential actions.
- **C — No AI:** Minimizes model risk but leaves useful assistance unexplored.
- **Why B:** Aligns opportunity with NIST/ILO/SIOP caution and JID's evidence proposition.
- **Reversibility/cost:** Medium; red lines prevent high-cost incidents.
- **Risk of delay:** Product team may implement inconsistent AI behavior.
- **Evidence required:** Bilingual evaluation, prompt-injection tests, source grounding, human review, cost and incident gates.

## FD-08 — Professional/social layer

- **A — General feed, likes, comments, followers:** Familiar growth loops; high noise, moderation, comparison, and strategic dilution.
- **B — No contribution features ever:** Simple but may foreclose useful mentorship and knowledge.
- **C — Bounded purposeful layer after wedge proof (recommended):** Projects, mentor articles, topic Q&A, cohort/event rooms, finite digest under explicit purpose/moderation.
- **Why C:** Preserves optionality without importing engagement-maximizing design.
- **Reversibility/cost:** Experiments are reversible; a social graph is expensive to unwind.
- **Risk of delay:** Low; defer safely.
- **Evidence required:** Task utility, moderation cost, abuse, comparison/wellbeing, privacy, and retention research.

## FD-09 — Monetization covenant

- **A — Advertising/data monetization:** Can subsidize users but corrupts trust and incentives.
- **B — Individual subscription-first:** Simple, may paywall canonical career access and face low-frequency use.
- **C — Free core + advanced Individual guidance + Business/University subscriptions and implementation (recommended).**
- **Why C:** Revenue maps to workflow value while preserving portable evidence.
- **Reversibility/cost:** Pricing is reversible; a data-sale reputation is not.
- **Risk of delay:** Pilot terms and entitlements remain incoherent.
- **Evidence required:** Paid pilots, willingness-to-pay, support and inference costs, renewal behavior.

## FD-10 — University product boundary

- **A — National outcome intelligence platform:** Ambitious, overlaps NLO/ETEC and risks authority claims.
- **B — Generic career-center CRM:** Crowded, weakly differentiated.
- **C — Outcomes and intervention operations aligned to official definitions (recommended).**
- **Why C:** Complements official institutions and connects Career Record evidence to University action.
- **Reversibility/cost:** Medium due to integrations and methodology.
- **Risk of delay:** Partner messaging may overclaim.
- **Evidence required:** Buyer/budget studies, data-governance review, reproducible cohort method, paid pilot path.

## FD-11 — Government posture

- **A — Fourth product actor:** Expands scope and permission complexity.
- **B — Replacement/official platform narrative:** Institutionally and evidentially unsound.
- **C — Authority/partner/customer context under explicit contracts (recommended).**
- **Why C:** Supports alignment and interoperability without implied endorsement.
- **Reversibility/cost:** High reputational cost if misstated.
- **Risk of delay:** Outreach may communicate contradictory promises.
- **Evidence required:** Named mandate, contract/API/data terms, and legal authority for each relationship.

## FD-12 — Assessment posture

- **A — Build full proprietary assessment suite:** High scientific, legal, and operating burden.
- **B — Integrate all assessment vendors:** Fast but outsources accountability and may import invalid tools.
- **C — Build workflow/work samples; integrate validated high-risk instruments (recommended).**
- **Why C:** Keeps structured decisions strategic while partnering for specialized science.
- **Reversibility/cost:** Vendor integrations are reversible; bad candidate decisions are not fully recoverable.
- **Risk of delay:** Employer pilot lacks a consistent screen.
- **Evidence required:** Construct/job linkage, reliability/validity, accessibility, Arabic equivalence, subgroup outcomes, human review and appeal.

## FD-13 — Capital and evidence gates

- **A — Fund twelve-month roadmap now:** Faster scope, higher waste under uncertainty.
- **B — Fixed 90-day envelope with staged release (recommended).**
- **C — Research only/no build:** Low risk, insufficient behavioral evidence.
- **Why B:** Produces end-to-end evidence while limiting sunk scope.
- **Reversibility/cost:** High reversibility at each gate.
- **Risk of delay:** Capacity remains unallocated and sequencing slips.
- **Evidence required:** Cost baseline, responsible owner, partner commitments, gate report, continuation criteria.

## FD-14 — Geographic direction

- **A — Saudi first; adjacent-market discovery after Year-1 gates (recommended).**
- **B — GCC from launch:** Larger narrative, multiplies legal/source/localization complexity.
- **C — Global English product:** Abandons the strongest differentiation.
- **Why A:** Saudi depth is the proving capability; interoperability preserves later optionality.
- **Reversibility/cost:** High reversibility before localization investment.
- **Risk of delay:** Low.
- **Evidence required:** Saudi retention, partner renewal, unit economics, portability, and market-specific legal/source research.

## Decisions that must not be bundled silently

Adopting the strategic direction does not automatically adopt the Constitution, authorize code, approve production access, approve a vendor, approve a partnership, or validate an outcome claim. Each requires its own accountable record.

## Immediate founder action

Record FD-01 through FD-07 and FD-13 before any implementation packet. Record FD-08 through FD-12 before the relevant pilot. Defer FD-14 until Year-1 evidence exists.
