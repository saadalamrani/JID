# JID Three-Year Strategic Direction

**Status:** Strategic direction under uncertainty, not a forecast.

## Destination

By the end of Year 3, JID should be a trusted career-evidence and opportunity operating system that connects Individuals, Businesses, and Universities through consented, traceable records and outcome workflows. Saudi Arabia is the proving ground; regional or global reach is earned through interoperability and evidence, not assumed.

## Year 1 — Evidence and repeatability

Prove the Career Record-led transition wedge, a bounded employer workflow, and a reproducible University outcomes methodology. Establish product-level economics, data governance, and an evidence culture. End Year 1 with a clear decision about which product boundaries are real businesses.

## Year 2 — Network utility without network harm

- Expand the employer and University network within validated segments.
- Add interoperable credentials/evidence sources under issuer and revocation rules.
- Mature Opportunity Graph quality, source operations, and partner interfaces.
- Add only purposeful professional contribution that improves a transition task.
- Standardize institutional onboarding and reporting.
- Test a second Saudi segment and one adjacent-market discovery program.

The Year-2 objective is not vanity scale. It is increasing utility from shared standards while each actor retains control and exit rights.

## Year 3 — Saudi category leadership and selective portability

- Become a recognized Saudi reference for transparent career-evidence workflows.
- Support portable records and opportunity intelligence across selected regional contexts.
- Offer mature Business and University products with auditable methodology.
- Establish ecosystem interfaces that let institutions and approved providers contribute evidence without surrendering user rights.
- Evaluate expansion only where legal, language, source, support, and unit-economics gates pass.

## Strategic bets

1. Evidence portability will matter more as skills and hiring signals fragment.
2. Trustworthy, explainable workflow can differentiate JID from opaque ranking and high-noise networks.
3. The three-sided system creates utility when the same evidence serves transition, hiring, and outcomes—without turning data into a commodity.
4. Arabic-first depth and Saudi institutional literacy are defensible operating capabilities.

These are **HYPOTHESES**, not established facts.

## Boundary over three years

| Build as core | Integrate/partner | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Career Record, consent/access, Opportunity Graph governance, Radar, hiring workflow, outcome methodology | Language/coding/cognitive assessments, credential issuers, selected opportunity sources, communications, analytics infrastructure | Emotion/facial/voice inference, black-box rejection, data sale, pay-to-rank, mass auto-apply/evasion, engagement-maximizing social feed |

## Measures of strategic progress

- Repeated successful transitions, not account count alone.
- Evidence reuse across actor workflows.
- Employer workflow completion and consistent decision practice.
- University outcome coverage and reproducibility.
- User comprehension, corrections, exports, withdrawals, and incident closure.
- Renewal, expansion, support burden, gross-margin path, and concentration.
- Source freshness and provenance quality.

## Three-year failure modes

Becoming another job board; confusing policy alignment with demand; building five unrelated products; letting enterprise customization fracture the system; hiding uncertainty behind AI; collecting data faster than rights can be honored; and expanding geography before Saudi repeatability.

## Decision posture

JID should be ambitious about the system and conservative about claims. At each annual boundary, the founder may continue, narrow, partner, or stop a product line. Strategic coherence is more valuable than preserving sunk scope.
