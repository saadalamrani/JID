# JID Validation Plan

## Purpose

Convert the re-founding thesis into falsifiable tests before broad implementation or launch. Validation is actor-specific and behavior-led. Interviews can explain behavior; they do not replace it.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Observed completion of a real task under realistic constraints.
2. Repeated use or paid/contracted continuation.
3. Operational evidence from partners and support.
4. Structured qualitative evidence, including non-users and failures.
5. Stated preference or survey response.
6. Founder/team opinion.

Lower levels can frame a test but cannot alone justify scale.

## Core hypotheses

| ID | Hypothesis | Test | Support signal | Failure signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | A canonical Career Record reduces repeated evidence work | Real record-to-application task against current workflow | Faster completion, fewer omissions, reuse on a second task | Users recreate data elsewhere or cannot trust evidence states |
| H2 | Explainable discovery improves action quality | Compare source/provenance/explanation prototype with list-only baseline | More qualified saved/applied actions and explanation comprehension | More clicks but no better actions; explanations mislead |
| H3 | Radar creates useful follow-through | Four-to-six-week private action pilot | Repeated updates and next-step completion | Notification dependence, abandonment, or anxiety without utility |
| H4 | Structured evidence improves employer consistency | One live requisition with anchored scorecard/work sample | Completion, lower rework, clearer decisions and documented overrides | Recruiters bypass structure or validity/fairness concerns dominate |
| H5 | Universities can produce transparent outcomes | Reconstruct one cohort report from source data | Reproducible E/K/N/coverage and accepted methodology | Missing denominators, unresolvable provenance, or unacceptable bias |
| H6 | Partners will pay for delivered workflow value | Priced pilot and paid-continuation conversation | Signed price-bearing agreement or paid renewal | Interest only when free/custom/unbounded |
| H7 | Users will accept bounded AI guidance | Usability, comprehension, error, and control test | Correct mental model, corrections, useful next action | Automation bias, false confidence, or inability to contest |

## Research cohorts

- Individuals: chosen transition segment plus non-completers and accessibility needs.
- Businesses: hiring manager, recruiter/HR, approver/procurement, and rejected/non-selected candidate experience.
- Universities: career center, outcomes/quality, IT/data, legal/privacy, and student representative.
- Institutional context: targeted expert interviews only; government participation is not assumed.

Sample sizes are set per method and decision. No universal minimum is invented; the report must state the number, recruitment bias, and what can and cannot be inferred.

## Assessment validation gates

Before any assessment affects progression:

1. define the job-relevant construct;
2. document content and scoring;
3. establish accessibility and Arabic/English equivalence;
4. evaluate reliability/validity appropriate to use;
5. monitor subgroup outcomes and adverse patterns;
6. provide human review and accommodation;
7. set retention and appeal;
8. prohibit emotion/personality/culture-fit proxies as automated rejection.

External tools require vendor evidence and independent review; marketing claims are not validity evidence.

## AI validation gates

- Ground outputs in approved records/sources.
- Show source and uncertainty where material.
- Test prompt injection and untrusted opportunity content.
- Separate recommendation from decision.
- Capture model/version/prompt-policy evaluation metadata without exposing secrets.
- Evaluate Arabic and English independently.
- Set cost, latency, quality, refusal, and incident thresholds.
- Provide a non-AI path for consequential actions.

## Outcome measurement contract

Every reported rate includes: eligible cohort `E`, known status `K`, numerator `N`, coverage `K/E`, value `N/E` or `N/K` explicitly named, observation window, source, deduplication rule, exclusions, last refresh, and limitation/bias note.

## Review cadence

- Weekly: defects, incidents, support, cost, and counterevidence.
- Biweekly: hypothesis status and scope changes.
- Gate reviews: Days 15, 30, 60, and 90.
- End-of-pilot: continue, narrow, stop, or redesign with signed rationale.

## Research integrity

Participants give informed consent and can withdraw. Incentives, scripts, raw notes, and analysis roles are recorded. Quotes are not converted into prevalence claims. Negative evidence is retained. Pilot success never grants permission to publish personal or institutional outcomes.
