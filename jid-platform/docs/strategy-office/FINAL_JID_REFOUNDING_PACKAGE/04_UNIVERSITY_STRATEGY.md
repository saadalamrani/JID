# University Strategy

## Strategic promise

JID helps a university **know what happened after learning, understand where evidence is weak, and turn graduate/employer signals into program and career-service action**.

A dashboard is only a rendering. The product is the governed workflow and methodology that produces credible outcomes.

## Users, buyer and approvers

| Role | Job |
|---|---|
| Career center | collect destinations, support students, manage employers/programs |
| Institutional research / data office | define cohorts, reconcile identities, assess coverage/quality |
| Program/college leadership | understand destinations, alignment and intervention needs |
| Quality/accreditation | document method, evidence and improvement actions |
| Executive sponsor | approve institutional value, budget and risk |
| IT/security/privacy/procurement | approve data, integration, contract and vendor controls |
| Graduate | control disclosure and receive direct career value |

The buyer and day-to-day user may differ. Discovery must interview them separately.

## University jobs-to-be-done

- define eligible graduate cohorts;
- contact and engage graduates appropriately;
- collect first-destination and later outcomes;
- know response/coverage and bias;
- connect outcomes to program and career-service actions;
- understand employer demand/feedback;
- manage internships/co-op and conversion where in scope;
- produce auditable, methodology-rich reports;
- protect named graduate data and purpose boundaries.

## Data readiness ladder

1. **Cohort contract:** eligible population, program, graduation period and identifiers.
2. **Identity reconciliation:** documented match rules, unresolved cases and no forced linkage.
3. **Consent/lawful basis:** purpose, audience, retention and revocation.
4. **Collection:** source labels for self-report, university record, employer confirmation and public source.
5. **Coverage:** known outcomes, response rate, missingness and subgroup distribution.
6. **Aggregation:** minimum cell rules, suppression and uncertainty.
7. **Insight:** program/sector/region/time comparisons only when sufficient.
8. **Action:** named owner, intervention and later evaluation.

No step may be skipped because the UI can render a chart.

## Recommended first product

A **Graduate Outcomes Pilot Workspace** for one institution and a bounded cohort:

- cohort setup and data contract;
- graduate invitation/consent workflow;
- destination survey aligned to a documented method;
- known/unknown outcome tracking;
- coverage and representativeness warnings;
- program-level report with suppression;
- employer feedback capture;
- action log for career center/program owners.

Do not begin with named-graduate employer access or cross-university rankings.

## Benchmark lessons

HESA defines eligible populations, complete/partial responses, response rates, collection timing and suppression. NACE defines a first-destination collection/reporting standard. These are methodological benchmarks, not Saudi rules. JID should co-design a Saudi-appropriate method with universities and authoritative local guidance, while preserving the same honesty principles.

## Commercial model

- paid readiness assessment/design sprint;
- annual institutional workspace license based on cohort/program scope;
- implementation/integration services separately priced;
- advanced benchmarking only after enough comparable institutions and methodology;
- public-sector or consortium work only under explicit agreement.

No institution should pay for access to private Individual data that the Individual did not authorize.

## Pilot acceptance

- signed data/purpose contract;
- one validated cohort definition;
- coverage reported with denominator;
- no small-cell or named-person leakage;
- at least two documented institutional decisions/actions;
- graduate-facing value demonstrated, not extraction only;
- procurement, security and legal learning captured;
- continuation decision based on use and quality.

## What not to do

- claim an employment rate from respondents only without coverage disclosure;
- treat nonresponse as unemployment;
- expose a graduate directory by default;
- rank programs/institutions without comparability and sufficiency;
- use salary/field alignment without definition and source rights;
- equate platform membership with the eligible cohort;
- promise accreditation compliance without authoritative mapping and review.
